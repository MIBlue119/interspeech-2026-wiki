# YODAS v3: Over 1 Million Hours of High-Bandwidth, Stereophonic, Multilingual Speech

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chen26d_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chen26d_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-386)

**TL;DR** — YODAS v3 introduces a weakly-labeled, multilingual open speech corpus of over 1.1 million hours of 48 kHz multi-channel audio across 147 languages.

## Problem

Existing open-source speech corpora are predominantly monaural, low-sample-rate (16-24 kHz), and heavily skewed toward high-resource languages like English, which hinders research in high-fidelity spatial audio, neural audio coding, and multi-speaker applications. Addressing these gaps requires a massive, high-resolution, and language-balanced open dataset.

## Method

The corpus collects 48 kHz multi-channel OPUS files and metadata from Creative Commons-licensed YouTube videos using a language-balanced search methodology. Instead of a shared global keyword list biased toward English, language-specific keyword lists are generated by filtering Wikipedia dumps and selecting top terms via a unigram tokenizer. Weak supervision includes original-language manual or automatic transcripts, English translations, and video descriptions timestamped at the utterance level. Baseline models for automatic speech recognition (using OWSM v4 base 102M with CTC segmentation and quantile filtering) and neural audio codec modeling are trained using ESPnet.

## Results

YODAS v3 contains over 1.1 million hours of audio across 147 languages, with 22 languages exceeding 10K hours and 73 exceeding 5K hours. Over 92% of recordings exceed a true estimated effective bandwidth of 32 kHz, and 71% (780K hours) are truly multi-channel (primarily stereo). Unlike prior versions, lowering the CTC score filtering threshold (retaining more raw data) consistently improves ASR word error rates across tested Common Voice evaluation languages.

## Applications

Speech and machine learning engineers working on multilingual automatic speech recognition, high-fidelity neural audio codecs, spatial audio processing, and expressive text-to-speech.

## Limitations

Automatic language identification is approximated using YouTube-provided video locales rather than verification via a dedicated audio language identification model at scale.
