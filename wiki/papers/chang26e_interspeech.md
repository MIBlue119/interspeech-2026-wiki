# Personalized Electrolaryngeal Voice Conversion with a Single Pre-operative Utterance

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chang26e_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chang26e_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1942)

**TL;DR** — This paper establishes a benchmark for one-shot personalized electrolaryngeal voice conversion using a cascaded framework that separates intelligibility restoration from timbre transfer, successfully outperforming supervised baselines in speaker similarity and perceptual quality.

## Problem

Electrolaryngeal speech generated by laryngectomees is robotic, low in naturalness, and poor in intelligibility. While voice conversion can restore naturalness, personalized systems typically require extensive pre-operative natural recordings, which are rarely available in real clinical workflows. Existing zero-shot voice conversion models fail when applied directly to electrolaryngeal speech due to severe domain mismatch, leaving a gap for extreme low-resource personalized restoration.

## Method

The study investigates two one-shot strategies using a single pre-operative utterance: a pseudo-target strategy generating synthetic training data for a supervised model, and a cascaded approach. The cascaded framework first restores intelligibility using locally linear embedding-based electrolaryngeal voice conversion (LLE-ELVC) with Chinese-HuBERT and WavLM features, followed by zero- or one-shot voice conversion models (FreeVC, Vevo, or Seed-VC). To optimize performance, the authors introduce a feature-level cascade bypassing waveform synthesis artifacts and a speaker-dependent supervised fine-tuning step using dynamic time warping to align source features with pseudo-targets.

## Results

Evaluated on four simulated speaker pairs (3 male, 1 female) using 240 training utterances from the TMHINT Mandarin dataset, the proposed refined feature-level cascade with Seed-VC achieves a Syllable Error Rate of 59.65% (surpassing the supervised ELVC baseline at 61.98%), a speaker similarity of 0.84 (vs. 0.75 baseline), and a UTMOS perceptual quality score of 2.30 (vs. 1.86 baseline). Subjective ABX testing demonstrates a 77.71% win rate for the cascaded system over the supervised baseline in speaker similarity.

## Limitations

Evaluated on simulated one-shot data from non-laryngectomized speakers rather than real clinical pre-operative recordings.
