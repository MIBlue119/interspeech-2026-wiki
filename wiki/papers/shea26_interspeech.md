# Masculinity and Sexual Orientation as Predictors of f0 Variation in the Speech of Australian English Speaking Men

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/shea26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/shea26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1906)

**TL;DR** — An empirical study of Australian English-speaking men demonstrates that sexual orientation and orientation toward traditional masculinity do not impact local fundamental frequency, but significantly affect f0 dynamicity and range through complex interactions with age and conversational topic.

## Problem

While the relationship between male sexual orientation and fundamental frequency has been widely studied, findings lack consensus, and the combined impacts of sexual orientation, adherence to traditional masculinity, and topic-based style shifting have not been investigated in the Australian English (AusE) context. Understanding these sociophonetic variables is crucial for uncovering how intersecting social identities and contexts shape human speech production rather than relying on oversimplified monolithic generalizations.

## Method

The authors collected speech data from 77 Australian English-speaking adult male participants (27 gay, 38 straight, 12 other minority sexual orientations) aged 18 to 52 using a Zoom H6 recorder at 44.1 kHz via a picture description task involving 6 neutral and 3 LGBTQ-themed photographs. Participants completed demographic questionnaires and the 8-item Male Roles Attitude Scale (MRAS) to gauge their agreement with traditional male gender roles, yielding z-scored MRAS scores. Vowels and sonorant consonants were filtered and processed through MacReaper to calculate glottal closure instants, local f0 estimates at 10 ms intervals, f0 differences between every fifth estimate within utterances, and f0 range in Hz. Linear mixed effects regression models were constructed with random slopes for topic on random intercept for speaker, using model simplification based on likelihood ratio tests.

## Results

Neither sexual orientation nor MRAS score significantly affected local f0, which was only impacted by age bracket where participants over 30 had lower f0 than those 30 and under (Est = -7.327, p = 0.026). For f0 differences, older speakers (>30) exhibited decreased dynamicity with increasing MRAS scores (Est = -2.805, p = 0.001), an effect absent in the younger bracket. Gay and bi+ speakers showed greater f0 differences (p = 0.005) and f0 ranges (p < 0.001) when describing LGBTQ-themed topics compared to neutral topics, whereas straight speakers showed no significant topic-based differences.

## Applications

## Limitations

The minority sexual orientation group ('other') had a limited distribution across age ranges, necessitating collapsing sexual orientation into a binary variable (gay/bi+ vs. straight).
