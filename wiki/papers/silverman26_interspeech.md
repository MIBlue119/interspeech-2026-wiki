# Learning Self-Supervised Spatial Representations via Soft Acoustic Contrastive Alignment

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/silverman26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/silverman26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-641)

**TL;DR** — The paper introduces Soft Acoustic Contrastive (SAC) loss, a self-supervised geometry-aware regularization framework for binaural audio that improves spatial parameter estimation without ground-truth labels.

## Problem

Most existing audio self-supervised learning methods process single-channel inputs and focus heavily on semantic content, treating reverberation and channel differences as nuisance factors. This makes them unsuited for spatially sensitive tasks like direction-of-arrival or reverberation estimation. While supervised methods exist, acquiring precise spatial annotations is expensive and limits generalization to diverse real-world environments.

## Method

The framework uses a dual-stream Multi-Channel Conformer (MC-Conformer) with a spatial encoder (three blocks, D=256) and a spectral encoder (one block, D=512) optimized via a mean squared error cross-channel signal reconstruction loss. A parallel projection head attached to the spatial encoder maps representations to a latent vector, optimized using the Soft Acoustic Contrastive (SAC) loss. SAC constructs soft similarity targets via a Gaussian kernel over five analytical acoustic features extracted directly from the input signal: TDOA, GCC-PHAT peak magnitude, and low-, mid-, and high-frequency band coherence. Pre-training uses 50,000 unlabeled binaural utterances generated by convolving WSJ speech with simulated RIRs.

## Results

The model is evaluated across five downstream spatial parameter estimation tasks under linear and fine-tuning protocols using 17.6M parameters. Experimental results show that integrating the SAC loss consistently outperforms fully supervised and standard self-supervised baselines (such as cross-channel signal reconstruction alone) across various spatial metric evaluation settings. The model was trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPU, requiring 4.5 hours for pre-training and 3 hours per downstream task.

## Applications

Engineers and researchers working on spatial audio processing, immersive AR/VR systems, autonomous robotics, and hearing aids who need to estimate room acoustic parameters like TDOA and T60 from unlabelled binaural recordings.

## Limitations

Evaluated primarily on simulated room impulse responses rather than complex multi-source real-world acoustic scenes.
