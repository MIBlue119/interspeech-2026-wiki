# A Corpus-Based Study of Creaky Voice Production in English and Mandarin

**TL;DR** — A large cross-linguistic acoustic study of read-speech corpora finds men produce creakier voice than women in both Mandarin and US English, challenging the common assumption that creaky voice is primarily a young-women's-speech feature.

## Problem

Creaky voice has been extensively studied in varieties of English, but cross-linguistic sociophonetic research comparing it across unrelated languages remains limited.

## Method

The authors analyze read-speech corpora from 66 Mandarin speakers and 61 US English speakers, processing audio through segmentation, annotation, cleaning, and acoustic analysis, and measure F0, H1-H2c, HNR35, and CPP over the mid-portion of 5,874 vowel tokens using linear mixed-effects models.

## Results

Men produce creakier speech than women in both languages, contradicting the notion that creaky voice is predominantly a young-women's-speech feature in US English but aligning with other findings of more male creakiness; Mandarin results are discussed relative to recent work on social perception of creaky voice.
