# Prosodic Realization of Focus in Yi-Mandarin Bilingual Speakers: On-Focus Expansion without Post-Focus Compression

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26g_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26g_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-660)

**TL;DR** — Yi-Mandarin bilingual speakers successfully produce L2 Mandarin on-focus F0 expansion fully comparable to native controls, but completely fail to exhibit post-focus compression.

## Problem

Post-focus compression (PFC) is absent in Yi (Nuosu) but present in Beijing Mandarin, providing a naturalistic testbed to examine whether L2 bilinguals can acquire PFC when their L1 entirely lacks it. Understanding this transfer constraint matters for modeling L2 prosodic acquisition and determining whether on-focus expansion and post-focus compression operate as functionally independent modules.

## Method

The study evaluated 21 native Yi speakers (L2 Mandarin) and 5 Beijing Mandarin native controls via an acoustic production experiment using 3-word sentences across initial, medial, and final focus conditions. Target words carried identical lexical tones (T1-T4) to isolate focus effects from tone-driven F0 variation, resulting in 4,662 total tokens recorded via Praat. Fundamental frequency, intensity, and duration were extracted, converted to semitone/decibel deltas relative to neutral baselines, and analyzed using linear mixed-effects models. Individual Mandarin language background predictors (exposure percentage, age of onset, proficiency, and current use) were tested separately as z-scored covariates.

## Results

Yi speakers produced on-focus F0 expansion comparable to native Beijing controls (group differences non-significant at W1, W2, W3 in-focus positions; p > .13), but showed near-zero or positive deltas at post-focus positions instead of the canonical compression seen in native controls (estimates -1.33 to -2.48 st; all p < .001). This dissociation replicated across intensity (-2.70 dB difference at Initial W3, p < .001), while duration showed general speech rate group differences alongside post-focus reductions. None of the four Mandarin language experience metrics predicted post-focus F0 reduction among Yi speakers (p > .11), confirming that lack of L1 PFC completely blocks its emergence in L2 regardless of experience.

## Applications

## Limitations

The Yi participant sample size is relatively small (n = 21), and duration measures were confounded by general group-level speech rate variations.
