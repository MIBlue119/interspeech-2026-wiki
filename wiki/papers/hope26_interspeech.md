# Lived Experiences of Power and Agency: Achieving Voice Sovereignty in Assistive Speech Technology for Nonbinary Users

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/hope26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/hope26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2663)

**TL;DR** — This study examines the lived experiences, identity expression, and agency of nonbinary speech-generating device (SGD) users through qualitative surveys, highlighting the technological and social barriers they face in achieving voice sovereignty.

## Problem

Nonbinary individuals who rely on speech-generating devices (SGDs) live at the intersection of marginalized identities and frequently encounter technological and social constraints that limit their voice autonomy. Current AAC systems often force users to choose between intelligibility and gender/identity alignment, while lacking adequate voice options, dialectal diversity, and customization. This lack of voice sovereignty exacerbates misgendering and psychological distress, underscoring the need for assistive technologies that genuinely reflect users' desired identities and communicative intent.

## Method

The study utilized an inductive thematic analysis of qualitative survey data collected from three nonbinary SGD users over the age of 18. Participants completed two anonymous, semi-structured surveys administered one week apart via Google Forms, allowing a week for reflection on their daily device use and identity expression. The survey instrument comprised eight open-ended questions targeting daily routines, challenging communication scenarios, scripting practices, identity limitations, and desired voice customizations. Themes and subthemes were systematically coded to identify physical, cognitive, emotional, and social integration factors, as well as broader issues of power and agency.

## Results

The qualitative analysis yielded three major themes: integration of SGDs into daily life, identity expression, and a cross-cutting theme of power and agency. Participants reported physical embodiment of their devices, keeping them nearby or mounted, but highlighted significant barriers such as extreme fatigue, environmental noise, and unaccommodating conversational partners who attempt to rush them. Regarding identity expression, users noted that available synthetic voices often forced compromises where intelligibility superseded personal identity representation. Participants explicitly expressed a desire for customizable, non-binary, gender-weird, or region-specific dialectal voices (such as Appalachian or New England accents) to reclaim autonomy over how they are perceived.

## Applications

## Limitations

The study relies on a small sample size of three participants, which restricts generalizability across the broader heterogeneous SGD community.
