# Toward an Articulatory Weakness Index for Speech Kinematics in Parkinson's Disease

**TL;DR** — A learned articulatory-state coordinate derived from articulography or acoustic data yields an interpretable index of articulator weakness that rises specifically in Parkinson's disease and tracks motor severity.

## Problem

Standard acoustic speech metrics mix respiratory, laryngeal, and articulatory contributions together, making it impossible to isolate articulatory dynamics specifically for conditions like Parkinson's disease.

## Method

The authors learn an articulatory-state coordinate from healthy electromagnetic articulography (EMA) data, where higher values indicate more restricted articulator motion, then derive an Articulatory Weakness Index (AWI) applicable to EMA or acoustic recordings.

## Results

AWI is elevated in Parkinson's disease patients and correlates with motor severity, while remaining stable in healthy controls and speakers with vocal hyperfunction.

## Applications

Could support non-invasive, acoustic-only monitoring of articulatory decline in Parkinson's disease, complementing existing respiratory and laryngeal clinical measures.
