# BEST-RQ-2: Contextualize-Then-Predict, a Two-Step Approach for Self-Supervised Audio Representations

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/tuncay26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/tuncay26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2488)

**TL;DR** — BEST-RQ-2 introduces a two-step contextualize-then-predict self-supervised audio pretraining scheme using a Vision Transformer, outperforming one-stage baselines across audio domains without increasing inference compute.

## Problem

Traditional speech-centric self-supervised audio models like BEST-RQ rely on Conformer encoders and in-place masking, which struggle to generalize across diverse audio domains such as music and environmental sounds. Decomposing masked prediction into separate contextualization and prediction stages enables better cross-domain transfer, which is critical for general-purpose audio frontends and large audio language models.

## Method

BEST-RQ-2 processes 16 kHz audio converted into 128-bin log-mel spectrograms, partitioned into non-overlapping 16x16 patches. A 12-layer Vision Transformer (ViT) context encoder (dim 768, 12 heads) processes only unmasked patches, while a lightweight 4-layer ViT predictor reconstructs masked targets using learned mask and positional embeddings. Training minimizes cross-entropy against discrete targets generated by a frozen random-projection quantizer with 8,192 codes and dimension 16. The predictor is discarded after pretraining, leaving only the context encoder for inference.

## Results

Pretrained on 1.9 million 10-second clips from AudioSet for 200k steps, BEST-RQ-2 is evaluated on X-ARES and XARES-LLM benchmarks. On X-ARES linear probing, BEST-RQ-2 achieves a mean-of-means (MoM) score of 0.50 (surpassing BEST-RQ's 0.43 and BEST-RQ (ViT)'s 0.44), with domain averages of 0.51 on speech, 0.41 on environmental sounds, and 0.58 on music. In X-ARES kNN evaluation, it reaches an MoM score of 0.38 compared to BEST-RQ's 0.25. On XARES-LLM downstream tasks, BEST-RQ-2 attains an MoM score of 0.41, outperforming one-stage BEST-RQ (0.33) and Audio-JEPA (0.35). Inference compute remains identical to the one-stage ViT ablation at 22.5 GFLOPs for a 10-second input.

## Applications

Speech and ML engineers building general-purpose audio encoders, multi-domain acoustic classifiers, or frontends for large audio language models (LALMs).

## Limitations

Training uses fp32 precision for ViT variants, and while inference GFLOPs match the one-stage baseline, absolute throughput depends on hardware precision advantages.
