# Vocal Tract Disparity and Potential Implications for Speaker Recognition

**TL;DR** — Geometric morphometrics shows male vocal tracts vary more in shape than female ones, offering a possible biological explanation for why both humans and machines recognize male speakers more accurately.

## Problem

Both human listeners and automatic speaker recognition systems show lower recognition accuracy for female speakers than male speakers, even with gender-balanced training data, and the underlying cause is unclear.

## Method

The authors apply geometric morphometrics to quantify sex-related morphological vocal tract disparity — the extent of shape variation — across resting and articulatory configurations.

## Results

Male speakers show greater vocal tract shape disparity in both resting and articulatory configurations, suggesting more discriminable acoustic signatures for male voices and a biological contributor to the recognition-accuracy gap.
