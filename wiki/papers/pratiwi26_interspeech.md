# Adaptation to Room Acoustics in Understanding Vocoded Speech: A Comparison Between Listeners With Varying Immersion Age

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/pratiwi26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/pratiwi26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-113)

**TL;DR** — This study investigates how listeners with varying language immersion ages adapt to room acoustics under cochlear implant (CI) simulation, finding that while intelligibility varies by immersion group, adaptation patterns over successive sentences remain consistent.

## Problem

While room adaptation helps listeners cope with reverberation, most existing evidence is limited to native speakers, leaving open how non-native listeners adapt to degraded speech under CI processing. Furthermore, speech intelligibility alone does not capture the cognitive demands or listening effort required during speech comprehension. Understanding how linguistic experience interacts with room adaptation and listening effort is crucial as diverse populations increasingly communicate in their second language under adverse acoustic conditions.

## Method

The experiment evaluated 51 participants categorized into early-immersed, late-immersed, and non-immersed groups based on their exposure to New Zealand English. Stimuli utilized the UCAMST-P matrix sentence test presented in two speech types: unprocessed (non-vocoded) and 8-channel noise-vocoded speech simulating CI processing across 400-7000 Hz. Five acoustic environments were recreated using a 31-channel loudspeaker array driven by higher-order spherical harmonic room impulse responses from a seminar room and a chapel at two distances, plus an anechoic baseline. Using a blocked presentation design of 10 consecutive sentences per room, speech intelligibility was measured via word identification, and listening effort was indexed via peak pupil dilation (PPD) tracked at 60 Hz.

## Results

Linear mixed-effects modeling revealed a significant three-way interaction between environment, speech type, and sentence order (chi-squared(36) = 146.57, p < 0.001), alongside an interaction between speech type and immersion group (chi-squared(2) = 43.48, p < 0.001). Under non-vocoded speech, intelligibility remained near ceiling across environments, whereas vocoded speech caused performance to vary significantly across sentences and rooms. PPD demonstrated comparable levels across immersion groups for both speech types. Notably, interactions involving immersion group and sentence order were not significant, indicating that trial-by-trial room adaptation rates were similar across different linguistic backgrounds.
