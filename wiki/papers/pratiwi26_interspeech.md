# Adaptation to Room Acoustics in Understanding Vocoded Speech: A Comparison Between Listeners With Varying Immersion Age

**TL;DR** — Listeners adapt to consistent room acoustics for vocoded (cochlear-implant-simulated) speech similarly regardless of their auditory immersion age, though intelligibility itself may still be affected.

## Problem

It's unclear whether adaptation to room acoustics when listening to degraded (vocoded) speech differs between listeners with different "immersion age" (duration of hearing-device experience).

## Method

The authors use a blocked-presentation design where sentences are presented within the same room acoustic condition to induce adaptation, measuring speech intelligibility via word identification and listening effort via peak pupil dilation (PPD), comparing non-vocoded versus vocoded speech across immersion-age groups.

## Results

Non-vocoded speech intelligibility stayed near ceiling regardless of environment, limiting observable adaptation, while vocoded speech intelligibility varied across sentences; PPD was similar across immersion groups for both speech types, and the overall pattern of change across sentences was comparable across groups, suggesting room-acoustic adaptation itself operates similarly regardless of immersion age.
