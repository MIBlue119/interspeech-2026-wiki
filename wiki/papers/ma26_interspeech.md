# The Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning Challenge: Evaluating Reasoning Process Quality for Audio Reasoning Models and Agents

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/ma26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/ma26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-118)

**TL;DR** — The Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning Challenge evaluates process-oriented chain-of-thought quality in audio models using a novel rubric protocol, attracting 156 registered teams across two distinct tracks.

## Problem

Current large audio-language models primarily evaluate final-answer accuracy, ignoring intermediate reasoning steps and resulting in a black-box paradigm prone to unverified or hallucinated logic. Assessing chain-of-thought quality remains difficult due to the instability and unreliability of standard LLM-as-a-judge metrics. To bridge this gap, the challenge establishes a rigorous shared task and evaluation framework focused on transparent, multi-step audio intelligence.

## Method

The challenge features two tracks: a Single Model Track for end-to-end large audio reasoning models operating in a single forward pass, and an Agent Track for multi-modal systems utilizing tool orchestration, planning, and self-verification. It introduces MMAR-Rubrics, an evaluation protocol that decomposes ground-truth reasoning paths into instance-specific binary criteria (k=5) generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro. GPT-4o acts as the rater to assess predicted reasoning chains against these criteria alongside a textual justification. Correct final answer execution is enforced as a strict prerequisite before awarding reasoning path scores.

## Results

The challenge garnered 156 registrations from 18 countries, with 23 and 24 teams successfully submitting valid preliminary results, and 14 and 16 teams advancing to the final leaderboard for the Single Model and Agent tracks respectively. MMAR-Rubrics achieved a Krippendorff's alpha inter-rater reliability of 0.7921, outperforming system-level evaluation baselines. The protocol also demonstrated superior intra-rater stability and closer alignment with human annotator preferences on 50 sampled reasoning pairs. Overall findings indicate that multi-modal agent systems currently lead in reasoning quality through iterative tool orchestration.

## Applications

Speech and machine learning engineers developing transparent audio AI assistants, multi-modal dialogue systems, and explainable audio reasoning agents.

## Limitations

Evaluation relies on proprietary LLMs (Gemini and GPT-4o) for generating criteria and acting as raters, which can introduce dependency on specific commercial APIs.
