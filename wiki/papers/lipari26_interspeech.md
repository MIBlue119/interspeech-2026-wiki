# Disentangling sociophonetic and physiological variation in /s/ acoustics across 12 languages

**TL;DR** — Using causal mediation analysis on a 12-language, 1,300+ speaker corpus, the study shows vocal-tract-length-based physiology consistently predicts /s/ peak frequency, but the relative contribution of physiology versus social gender performance to gender differences varies considerably by language.

## Problem

Sibilants like /s/ show gendered acoustic variation across languages, but it's hard to tell from acoustics alone whether this reflects performance of social identity or physiological differences like vocal tract length (VTL), and these effects need to be causally disentangled.

## Method

The authors use a new multilingual phonetic database of 12 languages (over 1,300 speakers) and causal mediation analysis to investigate the relationship between vowel-formant-estimated VTL and /s/ peak frequency, testing VTL's causal role in mediating gender effects on sibilant acoustics.

## Results

Estimated VTL predicts /s/ peak frequency well across languages, and while gender differences in peak frequency appear in nearly all languages, the relative contributions of physiology versus social performance vary considerably across languages.
