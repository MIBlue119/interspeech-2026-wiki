# Is Speaker Identity a Unitary Construct? Neural Evidence for Distinct Trait Processing

**TL;DR** — An fMRI study finds a shared voice-processing core in bilateral STG/STS handles all speaker traits, but gender, age, and accent each also recruit partially distinct brain regions and activation strengths — a "core-plus-extension" model of speaker identity.

## Problem

Speaker identity comprises multiple layers of information (gender, age, accent), but prior neuroscience work has largely treated it as a single, unitary construct, leaving it unclear whether distinct speaker traits are processed by shared or dissociable neural mechanisms.

## Method

The authors conduct an fMRI study investigating whether distinct speaker traits — gender, age, and accent — are processed by shared or dissociable neural mechanisms, examining activation in bilateral superior temporal cortex (STG/STS) and beyond.

## Results

A common voice-processing core centered on bilateral STG/STS is engaged across all trait conditions, but responses within this core are not uniform (accent elicits stronger activation than gender and age), and each trait also recruits partially distinct cortical regions beyond the shared core.
