# Lexical stress-conditioned spatiotemporal gestural coordination in L2 English

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/mcguire26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/mcguire26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3116)

**TL;DR** — Multivariate functional principal component analysis reveals that L2 English speakers with lower foreign accent ratings successfully implement spatiotemporal gestural coordination for lexical stress, while speakers with higher accent ratings do not.

## Problem

Taiwanese Mandarin (TWM) lacks a perceptually salient lexical stress contrast and its speakers rely heavily on F0 cues in L2 English, raising questions about how they organize supralaryngeal articulatory gestures across syllables. Prior articulatory studies focused primarily on vowel midpoint positions and individual sensor displacements rather than holistic, syllable-level intergestural coordination and its relationship to perceived foreign accent. Understanding these patterns is essential for speech engineers and phoneticians seeking to model second-language prosodic acquisition and pronunciation quality.

## Method

The study analyzes 98 usable tokens of the syllable /flIkt/ (from CONflict–conFLICT) recorded from 6 native TWM speakers using a Carstens AG501 electromagnetic articulograph at 1,250 Hz (down-sampled to 250 Hz). Sensors tracked lower lip (LL), tongue tip (TT), tongue dorsum (TD), and jaw (JAW) movements. The findgest algorithm delineated four primary consonantal constriction gestures, establishing six temporal landmarks from syllable onset to offset for landmark-based temporal registration. Multivariate functional principal component analysis (FPCA) combined spatial trajectories with time-warping functions to evaluate stress-condition separation without acoustic data. Perceived foreign accentedness ratings (AR) obtained from two expert raters on a 9-point scale provided descriptive context for individual differences.

## Results

PERMANOVA tests on PC1-PC2 score space demonstrated clear separation between stressed and unstressed tokens for 4 of the 6 speakers (p < 0.05, R-squared ranging from 0.240 to 0.416), while the 2 speakers with the highest AR values (01M with 6.0 and 07F with 6.5) showed no significant separation. Stressed syllables exhibited longer durations across all speakers, but syllable duration alone did not predict FPCA score space separation (e.g., speaker 01M had large duration differences but no score space separation). Articulatory perturbations associated with stress included greater jaw lowering during the vocalic interval, higher lower-lip position at the /f/ constriction target, and temporal expansion along the registered time axis. The two speakers lacking stress separation also exhibited abnormal temporal reversals in their consonantal constriction targets (e.g., L3 preceding L2, or L5 preceding L4).

## Applications

Speech scientists, computer-assisted language learning (CALL) developers, and pronunciation training system designers can use these spatiotemporal articulatory metrics to evaluate L2 prosodic proficiency and diagnose fine-grained fluency issues.

## Limitations

The study is limited by a very small sample size of 6 speakers and 98 total tokens, restricting the analysis to descriptive statistics and exploratory interpretations.
