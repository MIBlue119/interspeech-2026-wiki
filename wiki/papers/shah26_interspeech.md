# SingFox: A Multi-Lingual Singfake Detection Corpus

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/shah26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/shah26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2573)

**TL;DR** — SingFox is a large-scale, multilingual singing deepfake detection and source-tracing corpus spanning 126.32 hours across 20 languages, achieving a highest cross-testing accuracy of 77.84% using an LFCC-ResNet baseline.

## Problem

Current audio deepfake detection datasets focus almost exclusively on speech, ignoring the complex acoustic and musical attributes of singing such as vibrato, sustained rhythm, and F0 modulations. Existing singing datasets are limited by single-language bias (mostly English), a narrow range of generation architectures (such as only diffusion models or proprietary APIs), and a lack of text symmetry and source tracing protocols. This mismatch causes speech-trained detectors to fail when exposed to real-world multilingual singing attacks generated by diverse synthesis paradigms.

## Method

The SingFox dataset comprises 113,802 audio clips from 1,150 singers across 20 languages (14 international and 6 Indic) divided into six tracks covering global languages, Indic languages, instrument-based deepfakes, combined global-Indic evaluation, alternative fakes (real/fake vocal and instrumental mixes), and source tracing. Audio files are standardized to 16 kHz FLAC format and processed using dual normalization (peak and RMS) to eliminate loudness shortcuts, then segmented into uniform four-second chunks. Synthetic singing samples are generated using three GAN vocoders (HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN, UnivNet), two diffusion models (DiffSinger, DiffRhythm), two voice conversion frameworks (RVC, So-VITS-SVC), and one text-to-music system (MusicGen), with Whisper-large used for lyric transcriptions where needed. Baseline experiments employ an LFCC feature extractor paired with a ResNet classifier.

## Results

Evaluated on cross-dataset scenarios, models trained on CtrSVDD, WildSVDD, and FMC datasets achieved accuracies of 65.87%, 44.65%, and 77.58% respectively when tested on SingFox Track 4, while cross-testing models trained on various datasets achieved a highest accuracy of 77.84%. Model-specific objective evaluation showed that UnivNet-generated fakes were easiest to detect at 71.17% accuracy, whereas BigVGAN proved hardest to detect at 1.02% accuracy. Subjective evaluations via 51 non-native listeners yielded a Mean Opinion Score (MOS) of 3.468 for generated singfakes compared to 4.028 for ground-truth audio.

## Applications

Audio forensic analysts, security engineers, and speech researchers building robust, multilingual deepfake detectors and source-tracing systems for singing audio.

## Limitations

Subjective listening tests were restricted to participants familiar with known languages, leaving language-specific MOS evaluation for unrepresented languages as an open problem, and certain existing datasets like FSD and SONICS could not be fully included in large-scale cross-tests due to resource constraints.
