# UR-BERT: Scaling Text Encoders for Massively Multilingual TTS Through Universal Romanization and Speech Token Prediction

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/lee26h_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/lee26h_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-909)

**TL;DR** — UR-BERT scales multilingual text-to-speech text encoders to 495 languages using universal Romanization and a speech token prediction pretraining objective, outperforming existing G2P-reliant models.

## Problem

Current multilingual TTS text encoders rely heavily on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) toolkits, restricting their coverage to around 100 languages and failing to scale globally. Furthermore, text encoders pretrained exclusively on text corpora lack acoustic context, limiting their ability to model fine-grained prosody and phonetic alignment. This creates a severe bottleneck for low-resource and unseen languages.

## Method

UR-BERT replaces G2P pipelines with universal Romanization via Uroman to unify diverse orthographic scripts into a compact Latin alphabet inventory of roughly 30 characters. The architecture uses a standard BERT-base configuration with a character-level tokenizer and 12 Transformer layers, pretrained on 13K hours of speech-text paired data across 495 languages from FLEURS, Common Voice, and Omnilingual ASR. In addition to Masked Language Modeling, it introduces a Speech Token Prediction (STP) knowledge distillation objective. The STP target is generated by extracting 16th-layer features from an omnilingual ASR speech self-supervised model teacher (omnilingual-ASR-W2V-300M), aligning them to characters via MMS-FA CTC forced alignment, and discretizing them into a 257-cluster k-means codebook.

## Results

Evaluated using VITS backbones across high-resource (English, German, Mandarin) and low-resource languages (Afrikaans, Khmer, Javanese, Nepali, Setswana, Xhosa, Sinhala), UR-BERT consistently outperforms m-PLBERT and XPhoneBERT. On high-resource English, German, and Mandarin, UR-BERT achieves competitive or superior MOS scores (e.g., 4.35 on English, 3.88 on German, 4.95 on Mandarin) and lower relative UTMOS degradation compared to baselines. On low-resource settings, UR-BERT successfully synthesizes speech for languages entirely unsupported by prior G2P-based encoders while maintaining robust intelligibility and spectral fidelity.
