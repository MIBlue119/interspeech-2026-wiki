# Voice of India: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Real-World Speech Recognition in India

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/bhogale26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/bhogale26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3189)

**TL;DR** — Voice of India is a large-scale evaluation benchmark consisting of 536 hours of unscripted telephonic speech across 15 Indian languages, designed to address the limitations of clean scripted datasets and rigid spelling-based evaluations.

## Problem

Existing Indian ASR benchmarks rely on clean, scripted audio and public leaderboards that encourage dataset-specific overfitting rather than robust real-world generalization. Furthermore, traditional single-reference word error rate (WER) metrics unfairly penalize natural spelling variations and non-standardized renderings of English loanwords in code-mixed spontaneous conversations. This creates an evaluation gap where high leaderboard scores fail to reflect true performance across diverse regional dialects and telephonic conditions.

## Method

The benchmark was constructed using a population-proportional cluster sampling strategy across 139 districts, capturing dual-channel conversations elicited via tailored conversational prompts generated by GPT-4.5. Audio was filtered using WebRTC VAD, Meta MMS, SpeechBrain VoxLingua107, and DNSMOS to guarantee perceptual quality, while vocabulary-based inverse frequency weighting prioritized richer, more diverse linguistic segments. High-fidelity human transcripts were refined through a machine-assisted multi-annotator pipeline involving Whisper, IndicConformer, and an automated lattice-generation framework driven by Gemini 3 Flash to account for valid lexical substitutions and spelling variations.

## Results

Evaluated on 14 ASR systems (11 proprietary APIs and 3 open-source models) across 306,230 utterances from 36,691 speakers, results show that most models exceed a practical usability threshold of 20 WER. Sarvam Audio achieved the lowest WER in 13 out of 15 languages, though it still struggled on dialect-heavy subsets like Maithili (24.8 WER) and Bhojpuri (20.9 WER). District-level analysis revealed massive geographic performance disparities, ranging from 4% WER in Nainital to 44% in Mannarakkat, with higher error rates concentrated in parts of South India and North Bihar compared to the Hindi belt.

## Applications

Speech engineers and researchers evaluating and improving automatic speech recognition models for multilingual, code-mixed, and low-resource telephonic environments.

## Limitations

The benchmark is closed-source for private test-set evaluations (with only a subset publicly accessible), making full replication dependent on external API access or application via their project page.
