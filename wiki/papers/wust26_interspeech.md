# Applying the Simplified Vocal Profile Analysis to Swiss German Dialects

**TL;DR** — The first perceptual vocal-profile study of Swiss German finds gendered articulation differences (men more relaxed, women more pharyngeally expanded) and regional patterns like nasality in the Northeast, though rater agreement was low.

## Problem

Little is known about voice quality (vocal profile) patterns across Swiss German dialects and how they vary by geography and gender, and perceptual vocal-profile methods have rarely been validated on this variety.

## Method

Four coders applied Simplified Vocal Profile Analysis to recordings from 127 speakers (aged 18-40) drawn from the Swiss Dialäktatlas, and the authors examined inter-rater reliability and consensus-coded geographic and gender trends.

## Results

Inter-rater reliability was low (κ=0.09-0.39) but consensus coding showed clear stratification: men tended toward relaxed 'hypoarticulation' and women toward pharyngeal-expansion 'hyperarticulation', Northeast Switzerland showed more nasality and Central Switzerland more pharyngeal expansion (possibly confounded by sample gender balance), and creaky voice was rare overall.
