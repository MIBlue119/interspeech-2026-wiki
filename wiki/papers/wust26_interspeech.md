# Applying the Simplified Vocal Profile Analysis to Swiss German Dialects

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/wust26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/wust26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-329)

**TL;DR** — This paper presents the first perceptual investigation of vocal profiles in Swiss German using the Simplified Vocal Profile Analysis protocol on 127 speakers, revealing distinct regional clustering and gender-stratified articulation patterns.

## Problem

While Swiss German segmental phonetics are well-documented, the suprasegmental domain of vocal profiles has remained a major research gap explored only via impressionistic historical accounts. Understanding these voice quality distributions is essential because specific vocal settings likely function as salient geographic and social identity markers in Switzerland's diverse dialect landscape.

## Method

The study utilized spontaneous speech samples from a balanced subsample of 127 speakers (aged 18–40) from the Swiss Dialäktatlas corpus. Four coders performed perceptual evaluations using an adapted Simplified Vocal Profile Analysis (SVPA) framework across ten parameters, followed by consensus meetings to resolve discrepancies. Inter-rater reliability (IRR) was evaluated via Fleiss' Kappa (κ), and spatial distributions were mapped in QGIS using k-nearest neighbor (kNN) scalar interpolation.

## Results

Fleiss' Kappa showed slight to fair agreement across the ten SVPA settings (κ=0.09 to 0.39), with velopharyngeal settings yielding the highest reliability (κ=0.386). A gender-based split emerged where men exhibited relaxed vocal tracts (hypoarticulation) significantly more often than women (24 vs. 10), while women showed more pharyngeal expansion (hyperarticulation) than men (16 vs. 3). Geographically, Northeast Switzerland formed a clear 'nasality belt', whereas Central Switzerland was characterized by pharyngeal expansion (though partly confounded by sample gender distributions). Non-modal phonations like creaky voice were scarce, appearing in under 10% of speakers.

## Applications

Sociophonheticians and dialectologists studying regional identity markers, speech rhythm, and voice quality variation across German-speaking populations.

## Limitations

Inter-rater reliability was generally low to fair due to the subjective nature of perceptual voice quality coding, and geographic clustering of pharyngeal expansion was partially confounded by an uneven local gender distribution.
