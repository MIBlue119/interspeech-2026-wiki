# When 'yeah' means 'not quite': Multimodal detection of backchannels expressing incomplete understanding

**TL;DR** — Multimodal classification of naturalistic dyadic conversations shows 'incongruent' backchannels (feedback that implies understanding without genuine comprehension) are systematically separable from congruent ones, marked by more neutral head movement and lower acoustic dynamism.

## Problem

Multimodal backchannels are fundamental to conversational grounding but don't always transparently reflect the listener's actual cognitive state; incongruent backchannels, where observable feedback implies understanding despite none occurring, are a potential source of ambiguity that had not been systematically characterized.

## Method

Using acoustic, head movement, and discourse-related data from 45 naturalistic dyadic interactions, the authors investigate whether incongruent and congruent backchannels show systematically different properties via a classification task.

## Results

Incongruent and congruent backchannels are separable based on multimodal features; incongruent backchannels are characterized by more neutral head movement and lower acoustic dynamism, with discursive cues strongly influencing classification, suggesting reduced signaling effort for incongruent feedback.
