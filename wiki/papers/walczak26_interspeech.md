# From onset to coda: spectral variation in normative Polish /s/ produced by children

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/walczak26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/walczak26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1936)

**TL;DR** — This study analyzes how word position influences the acoustic properties of perceptually normative /s/ productions in Polish-speaking children, showing that word-initial and word-medial tokens exhibit the largest spectral differences.

## Problem

Sibilant fricatives are shaped by multiple linguistic and developmental factors, yet word-position effects in children's speech remain underexplored. Understanding these context-dependent variations is necessary because prosodic boundaries and coarticulation significantly alter acoustic realizations, even in perceptually normative productions.

## Method

The authors analyzed 816 /s/ tokens from 102 Polish-speaking preschoolers aged 5–8 years drawn from the PAVSig database. Four acoustic measures were extracted using 20 ms frames: spectral centroid (CoG), spectral skewness, spectral kurtosis, and spectral flux. Each feature was modeled via linear mixed-effects (LME) models with word position as a fixed effect, random intercepts/slopes for speaker (accounting for stress and adjacent phones), and random intercepts for parent words.

## Results

Pairwise comparisons revealed that the initial-medial contrast showed the largest effect sizes across all four acoustic measures (Cohen's d ranging from 0.65 to 1.09, p < 0.001). Initial-final contrasts were also significant (d = 0.47 to 0.56, p < 0.01), whereas medial-final differences were largely non-significant except for spectral skewness (d = 0.53, p = 0.02). Spectral centroid and spectral flux were notably higher in word-medial positions compared to word-initial positions.

## Applications

Speech-language pathologists, phoneticians, and researchers developing automatic speech recognition or clinical assessment tools for child speech.

## Limitations

The study relies on a small and unbalanced lexical corpus of only eight words, and age-related developmental effects were not explicitly modeled.
