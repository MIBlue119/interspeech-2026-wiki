# Effects of Co-speech Gesture on the Acoustic Realization of Focus in Cantonese-speaking Children With and Without Autism Spectrum Disorder

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/li26p_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/li26p_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1008)

**TL;DR** — This study analyzes how co-speech gestures (deictic and iconic) modulate focus prosody in Cantonese-speaking children with and without autism spectrum disorder (ASD), revealing distinct multimodal integration patterns between the two groups.

## Problem

While prosody and co-speech gestures jointly signal communicative focus, the degree to which manual gestures shape the acoustic realization of speech remains understudied, particularly in clinical populations. Autistic individuals frequently exhibit impairments in both prosodic focus marking and gesture-speech integration, yet how these systems interact during speech production is poorly understood. Addressing this gap helps clarify whether multisensory integration theories (such as Weak Central Coherence and Enhanced Perceptual Functioning) explain atypical prosodic output in ASD.

## Method

The authors evaluated 22 autistic and 25 typically developing (TD) Cantonese-speaking children performing a "Toy Talk" elicitation game that manipulated gesture presence (with vs. without gesture), gesture type (deictic pointing vs. iconic two-handed depiction), and focus structure (contrastive focus vs. non-focus). Audio was recorded at 44.1 kHz, and 6,016 target sentences (yielding 17,818 syllables after cleaning) were manually segmented in Praat to extract mean f0, f0 range, and syllable duration. Linear mixed-effects (LME) models were fitted with participant group, gesture condition, and focus type as fixed effects, and random intercepts for participant, syllable, and tone shape.

## Results

Iconic gestures significantly lengthened on-target syllables across both groups with effect sizes ranging from d = 0.39 to d = 0.60, and expanded f0 range for non-focus target syllables (ASD: z = 4.55, p < .001, d = 0.35; TD: z = 3.17, p = .008, d = 0.23). TD children exhibited post-focus duration shortening (d = -0.17 to -0.14) and higher post-target mean f0 under iconic gestures (d = 0.15 to 0.16) to enhance prosodic contrast. In contrast, the ASD group showed no mean f0 gesture effects but displayed a distinct duration-shortening pattern for all syllables accompanied by deictic gestures (d = -0.46 to -0.21) and post-non-focus syllables with iconic gestures (z = -3.50, p = .003, d = -0.18).
