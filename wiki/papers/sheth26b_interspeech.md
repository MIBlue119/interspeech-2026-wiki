# From Academic Tool to Community Infrastructure: A Call for Indigenous Partnership in Speech Data Governance

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/sheth26b_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/sheth26b_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2489)

**TL;DR** — The paper critiques the ELSI speech data governance platform for concentrating control of indigenous child-centered audio collections among European academics and issues a call to action to co-design indigenous data sovereignty frameworks.

## Problem

Large speech corpora often collect data from indigenous and small-scale communities under researcher-led protocols, leaving communities with zero formal control, veto power, or benefit pathways. Existing sensitive data repositories like HomeBank and Databrary enforce researcher-centric access rules without offering structural authority to the source communities. This reproduces historical inequities where marginalized groups lack sovereignty over their own cultural and linguistic data.

## Method

The authors examine the current ELSI platform, which holds over 40 child-centered daylong audio datasets (including 10 indigenous corpora across multiple continents) using a three-tier permission model separating Custodians, Tool Creators, and Analysts. They map this architecture against the CARE and OCAP indigenous data governance principles to expose gaps. To resolve this, they propose adding a Community Custodian tier granting indigenous stakeholders veto power, access logs, permitted use definition, and dataset withdrawal rights.

## Results

The evaluation analyzes 10 specific indigenous and small-scale community datasets within ELSI (representing languages such as Ticuna, Yélî Dnye, Tseltal, Tsimane, Quechua, Mambai, and Kairui across regions like Peru, Papua New Guinea, Mexico, Bolivia, Vanuatu, Solomon Islands, and Timor-Leste). The audit reveals that while technical tiered access and legal GDPR compliance are established, ELSI currently fails all CARE dimensions regarding community authority, control, and collective benefit. The paper commits to allocating engineering resources to implement co-designed governance architectures and opening working groups with community partners.

## Applications

Speech researchers, data platform architects, and indigenous community language boards looking to establish ethical, sovereign speech data management and access control systems.

## Limitations

The platform currently lacks automated technical mechanisms for indigenous communities to exercise governance, and determining legitimate community custodianship remains a complex, unresolved challenge.
