# Eye and Mouth Cues in Audiovisual Perception of Mandarin Irony: Evidence from Eye-Tracking

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/xia26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/xia26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1544)

**TL;DR** — An eye-tracking study on Mandarin irony comprehension reveals that listeners allocate visual attention asymmetrically, focusing primarily on the eyes for ironic blame and on the mouth for ironic praise.

## Problem

While prosodic cues in irony comprehension are widely studied, the role of visual facial cues remains underexplored, particularly for asymmetric categories like ironic blame versus ironic praise. Furthermore, behavioral measures alone often fail to capture real-time cognitive processes, and the impact of environmental noise on audiovisual cue integration during irony interpretation is poorly understood. Addressing these gaps is crucial for understanding multimodal pragmatic intent recognition and cross-linguistic differences.

## Method

Forty-four native Mandarin speakers participated in an eye-tracking experiment involving 120 target sentences across four pragmatic attitudes (sincere/ironic praise and sincere/ironic blame) and three sensory conditions (Visual-only [VO], Audiovisual in quiet [AV-Quiet], and Audiovisual in noise with 8-talker babble at -10 dB SNR). Dynamic areas of interest (AOIs) for the eyes, mouth, and full face were manually delineated to extract proportional fixation durations and mean pupil sizes using an EyeLink 1000 Plus tracker. Data was analyzed using linear mixed-effects models examining the interaction between AOI, modality, and attitude.

## Results

For correctly interpreted ironic blame, participants exhibited significantly longer fixations on the eyes than the mouth across all modalities with comparable pupil sizes. Conversely, correctly interpreted ironic praise prompted greater attention to the mouth in VO and AV-Noise modalities, while AV-Quiet showed balanced gaze but larger pupil size during eye fixations. Incorrectly interpreted ironic praise trials showed a lack of distinction, with participants displaying equal attention to eyes and mouth even in the VO modality.

## Limitations
