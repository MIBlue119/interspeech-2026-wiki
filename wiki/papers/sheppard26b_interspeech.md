# Towards participatory speech dataset curation: A queer case study and conceptual framework

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/sheppard26b_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/sheppard26b_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2908)

**TL;DR** — This paper motivates and proposes a conceptual framework for participatory speech dataset curation by, for, and with marginalized communities, using the LGBTQIA+ (queer) community as a case study.

## Problem

Traditional speech data collection practices rely on top-down crowdsourcing that often results in participation washing and ignores the specific risks faced by marginalized groups, such as the LGBTQIA+ community. Because gender and identity markers in speech can inadvertently "out" individuals or expose them to technological harms like discriminatory automated content moderation and biased speech recognition models, standard extractive collection methods are fundamentally unsuitable. This lack of safe, representative data stunts the development of speech technologies that accurately and equitably serve queer users.

## Method

The authors review existing literature on AI harms, linguistic field methods, and prior participatory AI efforts to build a normative, four-phase conceptual framework for community-led dataset governance. The framework consists of overlapping and bidirectional processes: defining the community, formulating the project, establishing modes of participation, and ensuring personal autonomy. Drawing from co-design principles and knowledge-sharing practices, the approach rejects unidirectional data extraction in favor of continuous, iterative collaboration between researchers and community members at every stage of development.

## Results

As a conceptual and qualitative paper rather than an empirical evaluation, no quantitative datasets, model sizes, or comparative performance metrics are reported. Instead, the utility of the framework is demonstrated through a detailed case study of the queer community, synthesizing insights from prior qualitative interviews, mental health participatory initiatives like PARQAIR-MH, and community-led speech efforts like StammerTalk.

## Applications

Speech researchers, data engineers, and practitioners designing speech datasets, voice assistants, or text-to-speech synthesis models who wish to ethically and safely incorporate marginalized or vulnerable communities.

## Limitations

The proposed framework is currently presented as a normative infrastructure and conceptual guide rather than a prescriptive set of requirements, requiring future empirical work to iteratively refine and test it across various real-world speech communities.
