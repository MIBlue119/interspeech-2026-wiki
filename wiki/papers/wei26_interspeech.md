# Vowel Nasalization in Upper Airway Diseases: An Analysis Using the CUCO Database

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/wei26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/wei26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-46)

**TL;DR** — This study analyzes vowel nasalization in patients with upper airway diseases using the CUCO database, demonstrating that the A1–P0 acoustic metric and the high-vowel /u/ are most sensitive to pathological differences and surgical interventions.

## Problem

Most clinical speech analyses of upper airway diseases rely on general acoustic measures like F0, jitter, and shimmer that fail to capture the core physiological changes of nasal airflow restriction and nasal-oral resonance coupling. Furthermore, research lacks systematic cross-pathology comparisons, longitudinal tracking across multiple postoperative intervals, and evaluations of vowel-dependent sensitivity to structural airway changes.

## Method

The study utilizes the CUCO database containing 107 Castilian Spanish speakers across four cohorts: healthy controls, septoplasty patients, functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) patients, and tonsillectomy patients. Acoustic metrics A1–P0 and A1–P1 are extracted from target vowels (/e/, /u/, /o/) in specific V-N and C-V-N contexts across three time points (pre-surgery, 2 weeks post-surgery, and 3 months post-surgery) using Montreal Forced Aligner and Praat scripts. Cross-sectional group comparisons are performed using one-way ANOVA with Tukey HSD post-hoc tests, while longitudinal trajectories are evaluated using paired t-tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction.

## Results

Cross-sectional analysis of pre-surgery data revealed that A1–P0 significantly distinguished the septoplasty group from the FESS group for the /u/ vowel (F = 4.287, p = 0.007, Cohen's d = 0.693 against FESS), whereas A1–P1 showed no significant between-group differences. Longitudinal analysis showed that only the tonsillectomy group experienced transient post-surgical changes at 2 weeks post-surgery (e.g., for /u/, a significant increase in A1–P1 of delta = 5.29 dB, t = -5.218, p < 0.001, and in A1–P0 of delta = 1.89 dB, p = 0.012), which returned to baseline levels by 3 months. Septoplasty, FESS, and control groups exhibited no significant longitudinal or long-term nasalization alterations.

## Applications

Speech pathologists and medical engineers evaluating objective vocal resonance changes, surgical recovery, and nasal airflow patency in patients undergoing upper airway interventions.

## Limitations

The study is restricted to Castilian Spanish speech materials and a limited set of vowels and nasal phonetic contexts.
