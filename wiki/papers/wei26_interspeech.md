# Vowel Nasalization in Upper Airway Diseases: An Analysis Using the CUCO Database

**TL;DR** — Acoustic measures of vowel nasalization, especially A1-P0, reveal that tonsillectomy patients show transient early postoperative changes in nasal resonance that other upper airway surgeries don't, offering a speech-based marker of postoperative nasal function.

## Problem

Upper airway diseases can alter nasal airflow and nasal-oral resonance coupling, but most existing acoustic studies rely on general features rather than measures specifically tied to nasal function, leaving a gap for systematic vowel nasalization analysis.

## Method

Using the CUCO database, the authors analyze two nasalization measures (A1-P0 and A1-P1) across patients undergoing septoplasty, endoscopic sinus surgery, or tonsillectomy plus healthy controls, comparing pre- and postoperative recordings across pathology type, time, and vowel category.

## Results

A1-P0 is more sensitive to pathology differences than A1-P1, nasalization is vowel-dependent with /u/ most sensitive to resonance changes, and only the tonsillectomy group shows transient early postoperative nasalization changes, with no long-term differences seen in other groups.
