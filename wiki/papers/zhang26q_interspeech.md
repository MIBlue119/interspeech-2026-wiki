# Age-related Differences in Acoustic Realization of Aspirated Fricatives in Shaxi Bai

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26q_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26q_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1200)

**TL;DR** — This study analyzes age-related differences in the multidimensional acoustic realization of aspirated fricatives in Shaxi Bai, demonstrating generational shifts in temporal, spectral, and phonatory cues.

## Problem

Aspirated fricatives are produced through a complex mix of temporal, spectral, and phonatory mechanisms rather than a single cue, yet few studies systematically quantify how these dimensions vary across age groups. Understanding this variation is crucial for explaining ongoing sound changes and intergenerational shifts in speech production. This work addresses this gap by examining the Shaxi variety of the Bai language across different generations.

## Method

The authors recorded 30 native Shaxi Bai speakers divided into three age groups (older, middle-aged, and young) producing 16 target words twice, yielding 960 total tokens. Acoustic measures were extracted across three dimensions: initial duration ratio for temporal properties, spectral centroid difference (CoG at 20% minus 80%) for spectral dynamics, and H1*–H2* using VoiceSauce for phonation quality. Linear mixed-effects models were fitted with aspiration condition and fricative category as fixed effects, and speaker and lexical item as random intercepts.

## Results

Temporal measures showed robust aspiration effects for older and middle-aged groups (e.g., old group positive effects ranging from 0.63 to 0.75), which were largely absent in young speakers except for /s/. Spectral centroid differences were largest in older groups (e.g., middle group CoG diff for /s/ at 2.38) and heavily reduced in young speakers (0.45 for /s/). Phonation measures (H1*–H2*) showed the weakest and least consistent effects, limited mostly to the palatal fricative /C/ with a reversed effect direction observed in young speakers. Speaker-level profiles revealed diverse weighting strategies—some speakers being spectrally weighted while others were temporally weighted.

## Limitations

The young group had a very small sample size of only three speakers, requiring cautious interpretation of the age-related dispersion.
