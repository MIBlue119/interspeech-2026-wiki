# Speech and Video Biomarkers Exhibit Reduced Within-Subject Variability in Early Parkinson's Disease and Resistance to Placebo and Hawthorne Effects

**TL;DR** — Clinical-trial data shows speech, facial, and finger-tapping digital biomarkers for Parkinson's disease are more stable and less swayed by placebo/observation effects than the standard clinical rating scale.

## Problem

Placebo and Hawthorne effects are well-known confounders in Parkinson's disease clinical trials, inflating variability in standard scales like the MDS-UPDRS.

## Method

Using Phase 2 clinical trial data, evaluates speech, facial, and finger-tapping digital biomarkers for within-subject stability and resistance to placebo/observation effects, comparing them against the MDS-UPDRS.

## Results

The digital biomarkers correlate with the MDS-UPDRS at baseline but show lower variability and lower susceptibility to placebo or observation effects, demonstrating greater robustness.
