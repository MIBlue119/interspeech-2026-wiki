# Acoustic token admixture for joint speaker and content anonymization

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/golmakani26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/golmakani26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1949)

**TL;DR** — This paper proposes a unified acoustic token-space framework for joint speaker and content anonymization via per-frame admixture and NER-triggered span replacement, achieving a 42.54% EER and 3.73% WER on the VoicePrivacy 2024 benchmark.

## Problem

Speech recordings in domains like healthcare and legal proceedings must be anonymized to protect both biometric speaker identity and linguistically identifying content under regulations like GDPR and HIPAA. Existing methods tackle these channels in isolation and typically require full re-synthesis, which destroys valuable in-domain acoustic characteristics, prosodic rhythm, and voice quality. Furthermore, text-level paraphrasing or phone-code masking removes speaker and acoustic properties entirely or causes acoustic discontinuities at edited boundaries.

## Method

The framework utilizes a single BigVGAN neural vocoder conditioned on three streams: a linguistic token sequence, a frame-level F0 contour, and an ECAPA-TDNN pseudospeaker embedding. The linguistic stream merges two sources per frame using a scalar admixture ratio beta (default 0.7): Stream A uses frozen Whisper encoder representations quantized via an 8-stage Residual Vector Quantizer (RVQ) with trained LoRA adapters, while Stream B uses phoneme-conditioned autoregressive tokens generated by a 220M-parameter T5-base transformer from G2P-converted and CTC-aligned IPA phonemes and articulatory features. A cosine similarity gate (threshold 0.6) rejects misaligned phoneme-derived tokens to preserve intelligibility. Additionally, a named entity recognizer triggers NER span replacement by overriding beta and substituting sensitive spans with newly generated phoneme tokens. The models are trained on English and French corpora including Common Voice, LibriTTS, VoxPopuli, and VCTK.

## Results

Evaluated on the VoicePrivacy 2024 corpus, the system achieves an Equal Error Rate (EER) of 42.54% and a Word Error Rate (WER) of 3.73% at beta=0.7, coming within one point of the top challenge submission (43.23% EER) while maintaining a mean opinion score (MOS) of 3.84. On the speech editing evaluation set, the full system maintains an edit similarity of 0.959 across NER-replaced spans, demonstrating perceptual continuity and consistent pseudospeaker identity. Ablations demonstrate that increasing beta monotonically raises the EER privacy metric from 23.0% (at beta=0.0) to 42.54% (at beta=0.7) with only a modest 1.2-point increase in WER.

## Applications

Speech and ML engineers looking to share or reuse enterprise, legal, or healthcare voice recordings for downstream AI training and conversational analytics while complying with privacy regulations.

## Limitations

The system achieves a lower Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) of 40.11% on IEMOCAP compared to cascaded voice conversion systems, indicating that purely token-predicted speech struggles to fully preserve emotional prosody.
