# Measuring English and Vietnamese language input and output in an Australian preschool – A longitudinal study

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/tran26b_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/tran26b_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3213)

**TL;DR** — This study analyzes English and Vietnamese language input and output during a preschool heritage language program, finding that children's overall speech output more than doubled in the second program implementation compared to the first.

## Problem

While early childhood multilingualism provides significant benefits, there is limited support for maintaining heritage languages in early childhood education settings outside the home in Australia. This lack of structured support frequently leads to subtractive bilingualism, where majority languages completely overshadow heritage tongues. Quantifying precise language input and output in preschool settings remains methodologically challenging and under-researched.

## Method

The study evaluates a Vietnamese language exposure program involving 30 preschool children aged 3 to 5 years (13 heritage language learners and 17 additional language learners) and a native facilitator across two yearly implementations. Audio-visual recordings from Zoom sessions (totaling eight segments: two ten-minute and six five-minute blocks per implementation) were manually annotated using Praat to measure exact speech durations for English and Vietnamese. MATLAB scripts extracted time intervals, and a Bayesian regression model implemented via the brms package in RStudio tested five hypotheses regarding language use over time.

## Results

Across the analyzed recordings, children's speech output rose from 10.9% of total speech in the first implementation (IMP-1) to 25.3% in the second implementation (IMP-2). Bayesian hypothesis testing revealed strong evidence that children's overall speech increased (Estimate: 43.0 seconds, 95% CI [0.35, 86.3]), alongside very strong evidence that both facilitator English input and children's English output increased. Conversely, there was very strong evidence that the facilitator decreased her Vietnamese input, and moderate evidence that children's Vietnamese output increased.

## Applications

## Limitations

The study relies on a relatively small sample of eight selected audio segments per implementation from a single preschool program.
