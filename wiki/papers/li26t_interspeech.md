# Hearing Smiles in the Crowd: How Babble Noise Shapes Smiled Speech Perception

**TL;DR** — A perceptual study shows that background babble noise not only reduces listeners' ability to detect smiled speech but also shifts their response bias, including flipping their default guess between amused and mechanical smile types.

## Problem

It was unclear how everyday background noise affects listeners' ability to perceive and categorize smile-like qualities in speech, which matters for understanding affective communication in noisy real-world settings.

## Method

Listeners performed two tasks under varying levels of multi-talker babble noise: detecting smile-like versus neutral speech, and, within smile-like speech, categorizing it as amused versus mechanical spread-lip speech.

## Results

Increasing noise reduced perceptual sensitivity in both tasks; participants became both less accurate and less likely to report hearing a smile as noise rose, and their default bias toward 'amused' responses in quiet reversed under strong noise.
