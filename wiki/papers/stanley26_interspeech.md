# Beta Rebound as a Neural Signature for Speech Movement: Preliminary Evidence Using Magnetoencephalography

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/stanley26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/stanley26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2995)

**TL;DR** — This study uses magnetoencephalography (MEG) to characterize post-movement beta rebound (PMBR) during natural speech production, showing that higher articulatory phrase complexity elicits stronger neural modulation.

## Problem

While post-movement beta rebound (PMBR) is widely studied for limb movements as a marker of motor inhibition and neurological health, its characteristics during natural speech production remain poorly understood. Investigating how phonetic and phonological complexity modulates PMBR is critical for advancing speech motor control research and improving clinical neurotechnologies like speech brain-computer interfaces (BCIs).

## Method

Five healthy adult native English speakers performed a delayed overt reading task featuring five distinct AAC-relevant phrases ranked by articulatory complexity using syllable count, duration, and Word Complexity Measure (WCM) scores. Neuromagnetic signals were recorded using a 306-channel Elekta Triux Neuromag MEG system alongside real-time audio and jaw pressure sensors. Preprocessed data underwent Synthetic Aperture Magnetometry (SAM) beamforming to suppress artifacts and localize volumetric beta band modulations from 13-30 Hz across a time-frequency region of interest (0.25-1.25s post-movement offset). Source-level time series and time-frequency response (TFR) plots were generated for the oral motor cortex.

## Results

Time-frequency analysis revealed that all spoken phrases elicited a clear bilateral PMBR in the oral motor cortex following jaw movement offset. Group-level comparisons showed that high-complexity phrases (e.g., "Do you understand me?") elicited stronger PMBR modulation than low-complexity phrases (e.g., "Goodbye."). Surface-level localization demonstrated a bilateral response with longer duration, stronger amplitude, and earlier onset (0-0.5s post-offset) in the language-dominant left hemisphere compared to the right.

## Applications

Speech engineers and neuroscientists can use these findings to design advanced speech-BCIs, improve pre-surgical brain mapping, and develop biomarkers for motor speech disorders such as ALS.

## Limitations

The study is limited by a very small pilot sample size of five participants and potential baseline contamination from residual long-lasting beta band modulations.
