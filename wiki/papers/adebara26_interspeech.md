# WazobiaSpeech: A Large-Scale Multilingual Speech Corpus for Robust and Fair ASR in Four Nigerian Languages

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/adebara26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/adebara26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3519)

**TL;DR** — WazobiaSpeech is a large-scale, ethically governed speech corpus spanning 2,540.8 hours across four Nigerian languages to support robust automatic speech recognition and fairness evaluations.

## Problem

African languages suffer from a severe scarcity of large-scale speech resources, particularly for spontaneous speech reflecting real-world conditions like code-switching and dialectal variation. Existing datasets predominantly rely on read speech and lack demographic metadata, hindering the development of fair, robust, and generalizable speech technologies.

## Method

The corpus collects 2,540.8 hours of audio from 2,865 speakers across Hausa, Igbo, Naijá, and Yorùbá using multimodal elicitation techniques including image and video prompts alongside scripted text. Approximately 96% of the data is spontaneous speech while 4% consists of scripted recordings covering specialized domains such as healthcare, agriculture, and financial inclusion. All data collection followed informed consent procedures, human-in-the-loop anonymization, and was transcribed by native speakers.

## Results

The paper establishes baseline automatic speech recognition results and cross-lingual error analyses, incorporating a fine-grained evaluation of tone sensitivity for Yorùbá. The dataset scales up coverage compared to prior benchmarks like FLEURS and Ìròyı̀nSpeech, providing comprehensive demographic metadata across 2,865 speakers. Detailed error breakdowns highlight the acoustic and lexical challenges posed by spontaneous conversational phenomena in low-resource African languages.

## Applications

Speech and ML engineers building automatic speech recognition, text-to-speech, and spoken language understanding systems targeted at Nigerian and broader African linguistic contexts.

## Limitations

The dataset focuses on four specific Nigerian languages, and while it covers diverse regional accents, its primary geographic scope remains centered on Nigeria.
