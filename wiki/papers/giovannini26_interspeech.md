# Exploring the Effect of the Visual Channel in Vocal Expression of Affect in an Irish (Gaelic) Synthetic Voice

**TL;DR** — A perception study combining a synthesized Irish-Gaelic emotional voice with matching or mismatching facial expressions finds the vocal channel dominates emotion perception, but conflicting visual cues still measurably shift listener judgments.

## Problem

It's unclear how much a synthetic talking face's visual expression affects how listeners perceive the emotion conveyed by synthesized speech, especially for an under-studied language like Irish (Gaelic).

## Method

The authors create vocal stimuli for five affects (angry, happy, sad, bored, relaxed) via voice-source modifications to a synthetic Irish utterance, pair them with facial expressions for the same affects plus "interested," and run a perception test comparing voice-only, congruent audiovisual, and incongruent audiovisual conditions.

## Results

Congruent visual context gave little measurable boost to perceiving the targeted affect, pointing to vocal-channel dominance, but incongruent stimuli show the visual channel still matters; visual information also improved listeners' ability to discriminate between high- and low-activation emotional states, largely confirming the second hypothesis.
