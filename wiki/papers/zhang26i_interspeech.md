# Tense Voice, Not Falsetto: An F0-specific Physiological Byproduct of Extreme High-Pitch Tone in Kaihui Xiang

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26i_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26i_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-693)

**TL;DR** — This study demonstrates through acoustic analysis that the extreme high-pitch tone in Kaihui Xiang is produced with a tense modal voice rather than falsetto, acting as an F0-specific physiological byproduct of pitch mechanics.

## Problem

Lexical tones at pitch extremes are underexplored, leading to a long-standing debate over whether extreme high-pitch tones like Tone 4 (T4) in Xiang Chinese are realized as falsetto or tense modal voice. Resolving this distinction is crucial for understanding whether such voice qualities are independent phonological features or predictable physiological consequences of high pitch.

## Method

The authors conducted a controlled production experiment using 104 monosyllabic words across all six tones, recorded from 16 native speakers of Kaihui Xiang (totaling 3,328 tokens after filtering out artifacts and Mandarin-influenced pronunciations). Data were manually annotated in Praat and processed using VoiceSauce to extract F0, formant-corrected spectral tilts (H1*-H2*, H2*-H4*, H4*-H2k*, H2k*-H5k*), Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR), and Cepstral Peak Prominence (CPP). Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMMs) were fitted via R's mgcv package to evaluate nonlinear temporal trajectories and tone-specific differences across normalized time.

## Results

Results reveal that T4 exhibits a flattened lower-frequency spectral tilt (significantly lower H1*-H2*) and strong periodicity without turbulent noise, matching tense modal voice rather than the steep tilt and high noise of falsetto. When F0 levels converge at syllable onset between T4 and T6, their voice qualities are statistically indistinguishable, proving the tense quality is an F0-specific physiological byproduct. HNR and CPP measures showed that T4 occupies an intermediate position rather than extreme values, confirming that spectral tilt is a more reliable marker for this high register than noise metrics.

## Limitations

The study relies exclusively on acoustic evidence without direct physiological measurements, focuses on an older demographic (ages 42–80), and is restricted to sentence-medial carrier phrases.
