# The First Environmental Sound Deepfake Detection Challenge: Benchmarking Robustness, Evaluation, and Insights

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/yin26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/yin26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1599)

**TL;DR** — This paper presents an overview, dataset benchmarks, and results of the first Environmental Sound Deepfake Detection (ESDD) challenge, where top-performing ensemble systems achieved an Equal Error Rate (EER) as low as 0.30% under unseen generator conditions.

## Problem

While deepfake detection for human speech and singing voices is well-studied, environmental sound deepfake detection (ESDD) remains underexplored despite rapid advances in text-to-audio, audio-to-audio, and video-to-audio generative models. Environmental audio introduces unique complexities due to diverse acoustic sources, overlapping polyphonic sound events, and a lack of reliable phonetic or pitch cues. Consequently, standard anti-spoofing models fail to generalize when evaluated on unseen generative models or low-resource black-box environments.

## Method

The challenge utilized the EnvSDD dataset, comprising real sounds sourced from six public datasets and fakes generated by seven text-to-audio and audio-to-audio models (Track 1), along with a low-resource black-box track featuring video-to-audio generators with only 1% training data provided (Track 2). Top-performing submissions incorporated advanced self-supervised acoustic feature extractors such as EAT, SSLAM, and BEATs, paired with custom classification back-ends like BiCrossMamba, multi-head factorized attention, and text-guided cross-attention. Training recipes heavily leveraged targeted data augmentations (such as MP3 compression, loudness normalization, and cut-and-mix), domain adversarial training, class-weighted objectives to handle data imbalance, and multi-system ensembling.

## Results

Evaluated using Equal Error Rate (EER), the challenge attracted 97 registered teams and 1,748 submissions. In Track 1, the baseline BEATs+AASIST achieved an EER of 13.20%, whereas the top-ranked ensemble system (AHU) reduced this to 0.30%, demonstrating high robustness against challenging unseen text-to-audio models like TangoFlux. In Track 2 under low-resource black-box conditions, the top system (DFKI with BiCrossMamba and EAT-L) achieved an EER of 0.25%, drastically outperforming the BEATs+AASIST baseline EER of 12.64%. Ablation and generator-level analyses revealed that flow-matching text-to-audio models like TangoFlux are substantially harder to detect than audio-conditioned generation models, and that multi-model ensembling is vital for maintaining low error rates across diverse synthetic artifacts.

## Applications

Engineers and researchers in public safety, media forensics, and trust-and-safety compliance building systems to detect fabricated environmental audio, fake alarms, and manipulated multimedia content.

## Limitations

Current detection systems perform holistic clip-level classification rather than fine-grained component-level localization within complex polyphonic soundscapes.
