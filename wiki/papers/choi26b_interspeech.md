# ZeSTA: Zero-Shot TTS Augmentation with Domain-Conditioned Training for Data-Efficient Personalized Speech Synthesis

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/choi26b_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/choi26b_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1269)

**TL;DR** — ZeSTA is a domain-conditioned training and oversampling framework that enables data-efficient personalized text-to-speech adaptation using zero-shot synthetic augmentation without degrading speaker similarity.

## Problem

Adapting neural TTS models to new speakers with extremely limited target recordings leads to a severe trade-off when mixing in large amounts of zero-shot TTS synthetic data: while it improves phonetic diversity and intelligibility, it causes speaker identity drift and degrades speaker similarity due to domain mismatch. Resolving this domain discrepancy is crucial for practical low-resource voice personalization where target data scarcity limits standard fine-tuning.

## Method

The framework builds upon a VITS base architecture, utilizing a lightweight domain embedding (size 64) added to the acoustic generation module to explicitly distinguish between real and synthetic training samples based on a domain label $d \in \{\text{real, synthetic}\}$. During training, the text encoder captures speaker-agnostic linguistic representations while the acoustic decoder conditions on the domain flag, and real target-speaker utterances are oversampled by a factor of three to stabilize adaptation. At inference, speech is generated by conditioning strictly on the real domain. Experiments evaluate the approach using Fish-Speech and CosyVoice 2 as zero-shot TTS augmentation sources to adapt models on LibriTTS and an in-house dataset (YoBind).

## Results

Evaluated on LibriTTS and YoBind using 10% real data and 90% synthetic data, the proposed method recovers speaker similarity lost by naive synthetic mixing while retaining intelligibility gains. On LibriTTS with Fish-Speech, the speaker embedding cosine similarity (SECS) increases from 0.765 (naive synthetic mix) to 0.815 with domain conditioning and real-data oversampling, compared to 0.832 for full 100% real data training. Subjective ABX preference tests demonstrate that listeners preferred the ZeSTA system over the unconditioned baseline in 70.8% (Fish-Speech) and 61.8% (CosyVoice 2) of trials on LibriTTS, and in 66.7% and 60.2% of trials on YoBind, with improved naturalness MOS scores.
