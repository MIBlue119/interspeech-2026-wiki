# Effects of listener language experience, masker language, and cognitive load on word monitoring accuracy and response time

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chin26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chin26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2950)

**TL;DR** — This study investigates how listener language background, masker language, and cognitive load impact speech-in-speech recognition, finding that a same-language masker causes the poorest accuracy and response times while typological similarity and cognitive load show minimal effect.

## Problem

Speech recognition in competing babble is hindered by informational masking, but it remains unclear whether performance drops stem primarily from target-masker linguistic similarity or listener familiarity with the masker. Furthermore, the role of working memory load during masked speech perception requires clarification, as previous literature presents mixed findings regarding bilinguals and secondary tasks.

## Method

The experiment used a dual task combining a visual digit preload (low load with 1 number, high load with 3 numbers) and an auditory word monitoring task where 78 Australian English monolinguals and 27 Arabic-English bilinguals identified English target words within 16-word sequences. Stimuli were presented at -5 dB SNR alongside two-talker babble maskers in four languages: English, Swedish, Arabic, and Spanish. Data were analyzed using Bayesian negative binomial and skew-normal regression models to evaluate sensitivity ($d'$) and response times.

## Results

For monolinguals, word monitoring accuracy was higher in Spanish maskers compared to English (ER = 20.39, PP = 0.95) and Swedish (ER = 199, PP = 1.00). Response times were slowest in the English masker condition, which was slower than Swedish and Arabic for monolinguals, and slower than all other maskers for bilinguals (ER = 7999.00). Bilinguals demonstrated slower overall response times than monolinguals (ER = 26.12) but achieved comparable accuracy without showing specific interference from the Arabic masker. High cognitive load successfully impaired digit preload performance (ER >= 7999.00) but did not affect word monitoring accuracy or response times.

## Applications

Speech scientists, cognitive psychologists, and audio engineers designing speech recognition systems or assistive listening devices for multilingual environments.

## Limitations

The study utilized a word monitoring task rather than a full sentence recognition paradigm, and remote testing via consumer equipment may have introduced variance in listening environments.
