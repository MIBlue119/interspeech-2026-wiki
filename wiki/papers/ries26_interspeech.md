# On Entrainment in Semi-Spontaneous Multilingual Parliamentary Speech

**TL;DR** — A study of French-English Canadian parliamentary proceedings finds that speakers entrain (adapt) to interlocutors across interrelated acoustic-prosodic and semantic dimensions and across languages, consistent with primacy and recency effects, in a previously unexplored semi-spontaneous multilingual setting.

## Problem

Entrainment (speakers adapting to interlocutors) has mostly been studied in monolingual spontaneous or task-oriented speech, leaving open how speakers entrain in mixed-spontaneity, multilingual dialogue settings.

## Method

The authors study entrainment in the semi-spontaneous Hansard French-English Canadian parliamentary proceedings, examining previously unexplored acoustic-prosodic features alongside semantic aspects at multiple levels of temporal granularity.

## Results

Speakers adapt across inter-related dimensions of entrainment and cross-lingual language settings in accordance with primacy and recency effects, offering novel insight into planning and priming mechanisms in this understudied context.
