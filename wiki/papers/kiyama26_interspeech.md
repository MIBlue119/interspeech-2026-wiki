# Voice Onset Time Categorical Perception in Mandarin-Speaking People Who Stutter: A Zoom-In Nonword Study

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/kiyama26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/kiyama26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2907)

**TL;DR** — This study examines voice onset time categorical perception in Mandarin-speaking adults who stutter using a nonword zoom-in design, finding atypical access to phonemic categories rather than a global perceptual deficit.

## Problem

People who stutter (PWS) experience speech production difficulties, but because speech production and perception share neural mechanisms, they may also exhibit atypical speech perception. Prior categorical perception studies on voice onset time (VOT) yielded inconsistent results due to confounding lexical influences from real-word stimuli and coarse stimulus spacing. Addressing these gaps clarifies whether sensorimotor integration deficits in stuttering extend to low-level phonemic processing.

## Method

The authors conducted an identification task and an AX discrimination task using an 11-step, then 31-step, nonword velar stop VOT continuum (/ko55/–/kh o55/) with a zoom-in design concentrating 10 stimuli around the native category boundary (step z5 to z14). A baseline letter decision task tested basic motor speed. The study evaluated 22 PWS and 18 fluent controls (final analyzed sample: 18 PWS, 18 controls) using probit analysis for boundary metrics and linear mixed-effects models for reaction times and d' scores, alongside stuttering severity correlations (%SS via SSI-4).

## Results

Control participants showed typical categorical perception with significantly slower reaction times at category boundaries compared to within-category conditions (p < 0.0001), whereas PWS lacked this asymmetry (p = 0.142). PWS exhibited a trend-level rightward boundary shift toward longer VOTs for aspirated consonants (p = 0.060). In the discrimination task, accuracy (d') and overall reaction times did not differ between groups. However, within the PWS group, greater stuttering severity (%SS) correlated significantly with faster reaction times for within-category stimuli (p = 0.010) while maintaining stable accuracy, suggesting potential right hemisphere compensatory processing.
