# Categorical Perception of Mandarin Tones in Jingpo Native Speakers

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/wang26z_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/wang26z_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1602)

**TL;DR** — Native Jingpo speakers successfully categorize most Mandarin tones but fail to show categorical perception for the T2-T3 (high-rising vs. falling-rising) contrast, driven by L1 inventory gaps.

## Problem

As ethnic minority populations in China learn Mandarin as a second language, understanding how their native tone systems influence Mandarin tone perception remains a key linguistic question. Specifically, the Perceptual Assimilation Model predicts that L2 sounds lacking native counterparts will show atypical perceptual assimilation and discrimination patterns. This study investigates whether native Jingpo speakers exhibit categorical perception (CP) of Mandarin tones compared to native Mandarin controls.

## Method

The authors synthesized 12 tone continua (encompassing 6 tone contrasts with two reverse source-syllable directions) using the PSOLA method on recordings from a female native Mandarin speaker, resulting in 132 unique stimuli. A total of 27 native Jingpo speakers and 23 native Mandarin speakers completed identification tasks and AX discrimination tasks (yielding 264 and 648 responses per participant, respectively). Statistical evaluations leveraged binary logistic regression for categorical boundaries and linear mixed-effects models combined with Tukey HSD tests for discrimination peaks.

## Results

While Jingpo participants distinguished phonemic categories for most tone pairs, they did not exhibit categorical perception for the T2-T3 (Tone 35 vs. Tone 214) continuum, which the authors attribute to the absence of a falling-rising tone in Jingpo. Across several other tone pairs, Jingpo listeners demonstrated a significantly lower degree of categorical perception compared to native Mandarin speakers, evidenced by broader boundary widths and smaller peak discrimination rates. The evaluation utilized 27 Jingpo participants and 23 Mandarin controls assessed via independent-samples T tests across 12 continua.

## Applications

Phoneticians and second-language acquisition researchers studying cross-linguistic speech perception, tone assimilation models, and accented Mandarin speech training for ethnic minority speakers.

## Limitations

The study is scoped to 27 native Jingpo speakers with a uniform 2B Mandarin proficiency level and focuses strictly on monosyllabic lexical tone continua.
