# Word Iconicity and Phonological Surprisal as Predictors of Age of Acquisition

**TL;DR** — Iconicity robustly predicts when children learn a word, while phonological surprisal mainly explains how efficiently adults process words — a developmental dissociation between the two effects.

## Problem

Iconic words (whose sound resembles their meaning) are learned earlier by children cross-linguistically, and phonotactically typical forms are also easier for children, but this contrasts with adult processing, where unusual forms including iconic ones are recalled and encoded more deeply, raising the question of how iconicity and phonological unpredictability relate to word learning versus adult processing.

## Method

Using machine learning and regression analyses, the authors examine how iconicity and information-theoretic measures of phonological surprisal and entropy relate to age of acquisition (AoA) across the lexicon.

## Results

Iconicity robustly predicts earlier age of acquisition across the lexicon, while surprisal and entropy show weak, inconsistent effects on acquisition but substantially predict adult processing outcomes instead.
