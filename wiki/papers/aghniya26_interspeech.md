# GRATS : A Natural Multi-Speed Mandarin Dataset for Speech Time-Scale Modification Benchmarking

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/aghniya26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/aghniya26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1842)

**TL;DR** — The paper introduces GRATS, a naturally recorded parallel multi-speed Mandarin speech dataset for evaluating time-scale modification against authentic target-rate recordings rather than artificial scalings.

## Problem

Evaluating Speech Time-Scale Modification (STSM) typically relies on English corpora and artificially time-scaled references generated by deterministic signal processing. This approach fails for tonal languages like Mandarin, which require fine-grained pitch-timing coordination and exhibit rate-dependent prosodic shifts that artificial baselines cannot capture.

## Method

The authors collected GRATS (Granular Rate Adaptation for Temporal Scaling), comprising 7,500 fully parallel utterances (8.1 hours) from 25 native Mandarin speakers across five controlled speaking rates (0.5×, 0.75×, 1.0×, 1.25×, 1.5×) using a karaoke-style visual prompting interface without post-processing. The evaluation protocol tests two classical (WSOLA, Phase Vocoder) and three neural STSM systems (TSMNet, ScalerGAN, CLPCNet) in an inference-only setup. Performance is measured across multiple dimensions using Character Error Rate (CER via Whisper medium), PESQ, STOI, DNSMOS, syllable-level duration Mean Absolute Error (MAE) via Montreal Forced Aligner, and WORLD-extracted F0 correlation.

## Results

Phase Vocoder achieves the lowest CER across all speaking rates, whereas neural models like CLPCNet attain higher DNSMOS and perceptual scores. Duration MAE exhibits a U-shaped degradation pattern with higher errors at extreme rates (0.5× and 1.5×), and F0 correlation decreases under faster or slower conditions. These findings reveal that standard systems struggle to preserve natural rhythmic structures and pitch-timing coordination at extreme temporal scaling.

## Code

- https://grats.tw/

## Applications

Engineers and researchers developing and benchmarking speech time-scale modification, voice playback control, and computer-assisted language learning systems for tonal languages.

## Limitations

The dataset is currently restricted to read speech from Taiwan Mandarin speakers, and the study relies on objective metrics that lack subjective listening evaluations for naturalness and speaker identity stability.
