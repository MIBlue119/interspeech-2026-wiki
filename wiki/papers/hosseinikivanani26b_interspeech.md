# Speaker-Specific and Language-Dependent Temporal Organization in Bilingual Political Speech

**TL;DR** — Analysis of 400 sentences from ten bilingual Luxembourgish-French politicians shows consonant timing carries speaker-specific signatures while vowel timing is mainly driven by which language is being spoken.

## Problem

Speech rhythm helps structure persuasive speech, but most rhythm research studies monolingual English, leaving open how politicians organize timing when switching between two languages such as Luxembourgish and French.

## Method

The authors annotate 400 sentences from ten politicians for segments and pauses, compute rhythm metrics (means, variability, pairwise variability indices) separately for consonants and vowels, and use paired t-tests to quantify speaker vs. language contributions and test within-speaker language effects.

## Results

Consonant-based rhythm metrics retain speaker-specific signatures across languages, whereas vowel-based metrics are largely driven by language choice, with French tokens showing longer, more variable vowels and vocalic intervals than Luxembourgish; no robust language-by-gender interactions were found.
