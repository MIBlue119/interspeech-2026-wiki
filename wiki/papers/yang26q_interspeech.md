# The Perception of Korean Stop Coda Consonants by Mandarin Speakers in Taiwan: A Comparative Study of Proficiency Levels

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/yang26q_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/yang26q_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3292)

**TL;DR** — This study evaluates how Mandarin speakers in Taiwan perceive Korean unreleased stop codas (/p, t, k/), revealing an accuracy hierarchy of /p/ > /t/ > /k/ that is heavily modulated by preceding vowel contexts.

## Problem

Taiwan Mandarin lacks oral stop codas, allowing only nasal codas like /n/ and /ŋ/, which creates cross-linguistic interference and persistent lexical confusion when learners encounter Korean unreleased stop codas. Because these sounds lack audible release bursts, learners must rely on subtle vowel-to-closure transitions, yet it remains unclear how vowel context and instructional experience shape their perceptual identification patterns.

## Method

A four-alternative forced-choice perception task was conducted with 30 Taiwan Mandarin-speaking learners of Korean (17 beginners, 13 intermediates). Stimuli consisted of monosyllabic items combining three preceding vowels (/i, u, a/) and three stop codas (/p, t, k/, plus /l/ fillers) recorded by native Seoul Korean talkers. Participant responses were analyzed using Friedman tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and vowel-by-response chi-square tests of independence to evaluate accuracy hierarchies, confusion matrices, and group-level differences.

## Results

Collapsing across vowel contexts, learners achieved the highest accuracy on /p/, followed by /t/, and the lowest on /k/ (Friedman test χ²(2) = 36.07, p < .001). Preceding vowel context significantly altered confusion patterns: /p/ was best identified after /i/ and /u/, /t/ was best identified after /a/, and /k/ was frequently misperceived as /t/ after /i/ but as /p/ after /a/ and /u/. Comparing beginner and intermediate course cohorts revealed no statistically significant differences in identification accuracy across any of the stop codas.

## Applications

Speech educators and curriculum designers can use these insights to develop targeted perceptual training and vowel-conditioned minimal pairs for L2 learners struggling with unreleased stop codas.

## Limitations

Proficiency was operationalized via course placement rather than standardized exams, and the cross-sectional design cannot isolate longitudinal maturation effects from instructional exposure.
