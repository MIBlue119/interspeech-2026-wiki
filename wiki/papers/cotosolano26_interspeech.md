# Automating Sociophonetic Research in Under-Resourced Languages: A Case Study of Speech Rate in Cook Islands Māori

**TL;DR** — Using ASR-based speech-rate measurement across 7.5 hours of Cook Islands Māori, this study finds that younger speakers on some islands talk faster than older speakers, while other islands show no such generational difference.

## Problem

Sociophonetic phenomena like speech rate variation across age and region are typically studied through labor-intensive manual analysis, which is especially limiting for under-resourced, previously unstudied languages like Cook Islands Māori.

## Method

The authors combine fieldwork and online recordings into a corpus, then use automatic speech recognition to semi-automate the calculation of speech rate (moras per second) for 60 speakers across different islands and age groups; automatic age estimation from faces was also tried but had to be replaced with manual annotation.

## Results

In the Nga Pū Toru island group, younger speakers show a significantly faster speech rate than older speakers, but islands like Rarotonga and Aitutaki show no such pattern, a difference the authors tentatively attribute to varying degrees of language shift across generations.
