# Adaptive Hard-Pair Sampling via Curriculum Learning for Speech Separation

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/shen26e_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/shen26e_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3139)

**TL;DR** — The paper introduces an online curriculum hard-pair sampling strategy for speech separation that dynamically weights speaker pairs using a running difficulty matrix, improving tail-end performance and bottom 30% SI-SDRi without sacrificing mean metrics.

## Problem

Standard neural speech separation models are trained via uniform mixture sampling, causing them to underperform on rare mixtures involving speakers with similar timbres or heavy overlap. These hard pairs generate the vast majority of tail errors and undermine real-world robustness, but existing offline hard-mining methods require cumbersome pre-computation or alter the training distribution. The proposed curriculum approach addresses this gap efficiently on the fly, eliminating the need for offline pre-processing.

## Method

The framework maintains an online dense speaker-speaker difficulty matrix and a corresponding count matrix updated via exponential moving average (EMA) using negative SI-SDR scores observed during training. Mixtures are generated by first uniformly sampling an anchor speaker, then choosing the interference speaker via a temperature-controlled Softmax distribution over observed partners. A linear temperature decay schedule guides the curriculum from near-uniform sampling in early warm-up epochs (e <= 20) toward hard-biased sampling, reverting to dynamic mixing after the stop epoch (e > 100). The approach operates entirely at the data-sampling level with negligible computational overhead, requiring no loss function modifications.

## Results

Evaluated on Libri2Mix (100h and 360h subsets with dynamic mixing) using Conv-TasNet, BSRNN, and TF-GridNet backbones. With an initial temperature of 20, TF-GridNet improves overall SI-SDRi from 21.9 dB (baseline dynamic mixing) to 22.7 dB, while dropping poorly separated mixtures with SDRi below 10 dB from 27 down to 7. BSRNN improves from 18.8 dB to 21.3 dB, and Conv-TasNet gains moderately. Furthermore, the average SDRi of the bottom 30% hardest mixtures for TF-GridNet increases from 18.3 dB to 18.8 dB.

## Limitations

The method requires tuning warm-up epochs, stop epochs, and initial temperature parameters to balance easy and hard sampling phases.
