# Talker Discrimination and Identification in 7-12-year-old Children: Effects of Talker Gender and Phonological Ability

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/holt26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/holt26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3053)

**TL;DR** — Phonological working memory significantly predicts talker identification accuracy in 7-12-year-old children, while talker discrimination and identification performance are moderately to strongly correlated.

## Problem

Developing a clear understanding of sociophonetic processing in typically-developing children is necessary before investigating clinical populations with communication difficulties, yet open questions remained regarding how children handle male-male versus female-female talker pairings, the overlap between talker discrimination and identification subskills, and the influence of phonological skills on these abilities.

## Method

Twenty-nine native English-speaking children aged 7-12 completed remote online tasks using Gorilla Experiment Builder, comprising an AX talker discrimination task and a modified ABX talker identification task across male-female (M-F), female-female (F-F), and male-male (M-M) pairings. Standardized CTOPP-2 subtests assessed phonological awareness via elision and phonological working memory via nonword repetition. Mixed-effects logistic regression models analyzed trial-level accuracy, controlling for age and including random intercepts for participants and target talkers.

## Results

Discrimination and identification accuracy were both significantly higher for M-F trials than for F-F and M-M trials, with no significant difference between same-gender pairs. Phonological working memory significantly predicted identification accuracy (beta = 0.274, p = 0.007) but did not predict discrimination performance, whereas elision and age showed no significant effect on either task. Talker discrimination and identification showed a moderate-to-strong correlation on same-gender trials.

## Applications

Speech-language pathologists and developmental researchers studying sociophonetic perception and communication challenges in clinical pediatric populations such as D/deaf or Hard-of-Hearing children.

## Limitations

The study was conducted remotely in participants' home environments without strict acoustic control, and sample size was relatively small (29 children).
