# What Makes Us Hate Our Own Voice? Large-scale experiments on Playback–Imagery Gaps and Individual--Speech Feature Effects

**TL;DR** — A large behavioral study (N=459) probing why people dislike hearing their recorded voice, finding recordings trigger more discomfort than imagining one's own speech, with listener-specific acoustic sensitivities.

## Problem

Self-voice aversion, the discomfort felt when hearing one's own recorded voice, is often attributed to a mismatch between imagined and recorded self-voice, but the contributing factors remain unclear.

## Method

Compared impression ratings after playback of one's recorded utterances versus imagined production of the same utterances, using counterbalanced block order with 459 Japanese participants, and explored trait-acoustic interactions.

## Results

Playback produced more negative ratings, specifically more discomfort, than imagery, though not for self-likeness; the gap also varied with listener traits and presentation order, indicating contextual modulation.
