# Broad Focus Rise(-fall) Declaratives in Venetan: Investigating Typological Outliers in Italo-Romance Intonation

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/payne26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/payne26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2691)

**TL;DR** — An acoustic and statistical analysis of semi-spontaneous speech reveals that unmarked broad focus declaratives in the Venetan dialect frequently employ a rising or rise-falling nuclear tune (roughly as common as falling tunes), challenging standard Italo-Romance intonational typology.

## Problem

Cross-linguistically, unmarked declarative sentences almost universally employ falling intonation contours due to aerodynamic constraints and frequency codes, while rising declarative contours are considered typologically rare. Although anecdotal evidence or read-speech studies suggest that Venetan dialects might feature unmarked declarative rises, little is known about their actual distribution, phonetic properties, and phonological representation in spontaneous speech compared to regional Italian.

## Method

The study analyzed 1.36 hours of semi-spontaneous conversation and map tasks from 8 older, predominantly monolingual dialectophone speakers of gambellarese (a central-western Venetan dialect) aged 59–81. The resulting corpus of 269 clean broad focus declarative intonational phrases was categorized by lexical stress type (oxytonic, paroxytonic, proparoxytonic) and nuclear contour (FALL versus RISE/RISE-FALL). Fundamental frequency (f0) tracks were extracted, converted to semitones, time-normalized across a defined Region of Interest (RoI), and evaluated using Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMMs) to test the significance of contour type and word type.

## Results

GAMM comparisons demonstrated statistically significant effects for both contour type (difference = 122.874, p < 0.001) and word type (difference = 25.909, p < 0.001). RISE(-FALL) contours exhibited both a distinct contour shape and a significantly higher overall f0 pitch than FALL contours across the RoI. Oxytonic words showed a significant difference in contour shape and higher pitch than non-oxytonic words, with surface rises predominantly appearing on oxytonics and rise-falls on non-oxytonics, pointing to a truncation strategy to avoid tonal crowding.

## Applications

Phoneticians, sociolinguists, and speech engineers studying intonational typology, dialectal variation, and prosodic transfer in Romance languages.

## Limitations

The dataset is restricted to older speakers of a single Venetan dialect (gambellarese) to minimize Italian influence, and contains too few proparoxytone tokens to draw definitive sub-conclusions for that specific word type.
