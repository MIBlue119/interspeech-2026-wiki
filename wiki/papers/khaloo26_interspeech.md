# Perceptual and Acoustic Correlates of Racial Identity in Text-to-Speech Voices

**TL;DR** — Listeners perceive and stereotype synthesized voices by race, showing a bias toward hearing voices as "White," and a classifier trained on acoustic features can distinguish voices rated Black vs. White with 65% accuracy.

## Problem

Voice-AI systems are increasingly human-like, raising questions about how listeners socially perceive synthetic voices — including whether listeners perceive racial identity in synthesized speech and what acoustic cues drive that perception.

## Method

The authors run a perception study measuring whether listeners perceive a sense of speaker race from synthesized speech and whether they engage in racial stereotyping, then train a Gradient Boosted Decision Tree classifier on acoustic features to distinguish voices rated as Black from those rated as White.

## Results

Listeners generally perceive racial identity in synthesized voices and show racial stereotyping, with an overall bias toward rating voices as "White"; the acoustic classifier reaches 65% accuracy, and voices with lower residual H1* and different spectral tilt patterns, along with certain noise and formant measurements, are more often rated as Black.
