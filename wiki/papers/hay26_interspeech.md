# What does it mean to 'know a word'?

**Category:** `phonetics-linguistics`

**TL;DR** — A keynote talk arguing that word knowledge spans a far wider continuum than usually assumed, from implicit awareness of words in a language one doesn't speak to richly detailed phonetic/social memory for highly familiar words.

## Problem

Existing accounts of 'knowing a word' describe a continuum from passive recognition to complex contextual usage, but this talk argues the continuum should extend further in both directions than commonly studied.

## Method

Drawing on corpus and experimental studies of te reo Māori and English, the talk examines implicit knowledge of words in an unspoken but ambiently-heard language, alongside detailed phonetic and social information stored for highly familiar words and used in speech perception.

## Results

The work shows people can develop implicit knowledge that a word exists without conscious awareness (even for an unspoken language), and that familiar words carry rich phonetic/social detail that structures individual variation and informs listener perception.
