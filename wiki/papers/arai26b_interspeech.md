# Programmable Speech Synthesis without Computers

**TL;DR** — A purely mechanical vocal-tract device that can be "reprogrammed" to different phrases by swapping interchangeable cam plates, no digital computation involved.

## Problem

Mechanical (non-digital) speech synthesis devices built from physical vocal-tract models normally need to be physically redesigned for each new target phrase.

## Method

The device shapes a mechanical vocal tract using sliding blocks positioned by cams; the authors introduce a set of interchangeable plates with different cam profiles that can be swapped in and out to realize arbitrary block-height sequences, making the tract shape "programmable."

## Results

Demonstrates synthesis of multiple distinct speech phrases from a single mechanical device by changing which cam plates are inserted; the abstract reports no quantitative acoustic metrics.

## Applications

Teaching articulatory phonetics and vocal-tract acoustics, and low-tech/offline demonstrations of speech production principles.
