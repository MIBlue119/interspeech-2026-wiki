# Relative Importance of Formants to the Intelligibility of Vocoded Speech in Cochlear Implant Simulation

**TL;DR** — A perceptual study finds the second formant matters most for understanding vocoder-simulated cochlear-implant speech, echoing normal-hearing speech but modulated by vocoder settings.

## Problem

It is unclear which formants matter most for intelligibility once speech is degraded the way a cochlear implant sound processor degrades it, information that could guide CI processor design.

## Method

Mandarin sentences were reduced to sine-wave speech keeping only the first three formant trajectories, then each formant was removed one at a time (leaving two intact) to measure its contribution to listener intelligibility under CI vocoder simulation.

## Results

The second formant contributed most to intelligibility and the third the least, matching patterns seen in non-vocoded speech, but the size of each formant's contribution shifted with vocoder parameters like envelope cutoff frequency and channel count.
