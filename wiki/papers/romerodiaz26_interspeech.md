# Listening or Reading? Evaluating Speech Awareness in Chain-of-Thought Speech-to-Text Translation

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/romerodiaz26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/romerodiaz26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-800)

**TL;DR** — This study evaluates whether Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Speech-to-Text Translation models actually process acoustic speech information, finding that standard CoT heavily relies on text transcripts, but model modifications can improve acoustic awareness.

## Problem

Speech-to-Text Translation models using Chain-of-Thought prompting retain access to both speech inputs and intermediate transcriptions during translation, supposedly overcoming the error propagation and prosody blindness of traditional cascade systems. However, this fundamental assumption has not been systematically validated. The authors investigate whether CoT models genuinely exploit acoustic cues or simply act as glorified cascade systems relying exclusively on the text transcripts.

## Method

The research builds on SalamandraTA-7b-instruct, a 7-billion parameter Llama-based translation LLM, expanded with discrete speech units (DSUs) extracted from a frozen 25Hz mHuBERT encoder clustered via k-means into 500 centroids. It compares a standard CoT training configuration (Base) against two training interventions: Dual (mixing 25% CoT and 75% direct translation formats) and Noisy (injecting corrupted transcripts into 25% of CoT samples to discourage transcript over-reliance). Internal model mechanics are examined using Value Zeroing attribution analysis via the Inseq library, while robustness is tested by replacing transcript segments with semantically divergent, fluent fragments generated by Gemini-2.0-Flash at corruption ratios ranging from 2.5% to 30%.

## Results

Evaluations across Common Voice, VoxPopuli, MLS, CoVoST 2, and Europarl-ST translating English into six European languages show that standard CoT models behave almost identically to cascade systems and heavily prioritize transcript tokens over speech tokens. When transcripts are corrupted with noise, standard CoT translation quality drops severely, confirming its over-reliance on text. In contrast, the proposed Noisy and Dual training strategies successfully shift internal attribution scores to increase acoustic reliance and boost robustness against transcription errors. Prosody awareness is specifically measured using the ContraProst benchmark, demonstrating how these training interventions alter speech integration.

## Applications

Speech and ML engineers building Speech Large Language Models or direct Speech-to-Text Translation systems can use these findings and training strategies to design models that robustly integrate acoustic information.

## Limitations

The evaluation is restricted to translating from a single source language (English) into six European target languages.
