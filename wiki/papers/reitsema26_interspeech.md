# Returning the Turn: Do Backchannels Pattern More Like Turn-Holds or Turn-Changes Given Preceding Syntactic Completion and Boundary Tones?

**TL;DR** — Dutch backchannels turn out to pattern distinctly from both turn-holds and turn-changes in terms of preceding intonation and syntactic completion, though they resemble turn-changes somewhat more closely, challenging their usual classification as a turn-hold subtype.

## Problem

Prior work on Dutch intonation and turn-taking treated backchannels as a subclass of turn-holds, but this leaves unclear what specific prosodic and syntactic cues actually invite a backchannel versus a full turn change.

## Method

The authors re-examine the place of backchannels in Dutch turn-taking by analyzing the boundary tones and syntactic completion immediately preceding backchannels compared to turn-holds and turn-changes.

## Results

Backchannels pattern differently from both turn-holds and turn-changes on these cues, while showing a relatively closer resemblance to turn-changes than to turn-holds.
