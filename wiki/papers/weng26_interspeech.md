# Tone-space Distribution Modulates Transfer from Non-linguistic Pitch Training to Cantonese Tone-in-Noise Perception in Native Speakers

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/weng26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/weng26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1274)

**TL;DR** — Non-linguistic pitch training improves pure-tone psychophysical acuity and selectively enhances noise-degraded Cantonese lexical tone perception via vertical transfer, with generalization to novel talkers restricted to acoustically distinct tones.

## Problem

While native tone-language speakers exhibit superior pitch processing, lexical tone perception remains highly vulnerable in multi-talker babble noise due to informational masking and compressed tone spaces (such as Cantonese's six-tone system). It remains unclear whether domain-general, non-linguistic pitch training can sharpen low-level psychophysical acuity to drive vertical transfer and improve speech-in-noise tone identification, or if such training is fundamentally constrained by linguistic category structures and ceiling effects in quiet conditions.

## Method

Twenty-nine native Cantonese adults completed an 8-session online ABX pitch discrimination training program over 14 days using pure tones (126–217 Hz), alternating between level-tone and contour-tone subcomponents with an adaptive 2-down-1-up staircase procedure. Level-tone difficulty was adjusted via semitone differences (0.03 to 2 st), while contour-tone difficulty was varied using modulation-window durations (10% to 95% of a 400-ms window). Pre- and post-training evaluations measured psychophysical just noticeable differences (JNDs) using a three-interval oddity task, alongside word identification tests across clean, 0 dB SNR, and -5 dB SNR multi-talker babble noise conditions with both trained and novel talkers.

## Results

Oddity task JNDs decreased significantly from pre- to post-training for level tones (main effect of Session: F(1, 27) = 4.24, p = .049, eta_p^2 = .136) and contour tones (F(1, 27) = 35.00, p < .001, eta_p^2 = .57), eliminating pretest directional asymmetries in contours. Word identification accuracy improved markedly under noise-degraded conditions rather than clean conditions (except for T6). Training gains generalized to novel talkers at -5 dB SNR for acoustically distinct tones (T1, T3, and T4), whereas tones occupying crowded, overlapping pitch spaces (T2, T5, and T6) exhibited limited gains and failed to generalize.

## Applications

Speech-language pathologists, audiologists, and researchers developing auditory rehabilitation programs or perceptual training protocols to improve speech-in-noise comprehension for speakers of tonal languages.

## Limitations

The study is limited to 29 participants, focuses exclusively on native Cantonese speakers with a specific set of base syllables, and shows that non-linguistic training alone is insufficient to generalize across novel talkers for densely crowded, overlapping tonal categories.
