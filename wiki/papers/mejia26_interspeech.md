# Sound Reactor Mission: Gamified Misophonia Assessment to Bridge the Gap in Audiology and Hearing Care

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/mejia26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/mejia26_interspeech.html)

**TL;DR** — Sound Reactor Mission (SRM) is a browser-based, gamified speech-in-noise assessment tool that quantifies the impact of misophonia-triggering sounds on speech intelligibility, achieving a 100% completion rate in pilot testing with a mean duration of 6.5 minutes.

## Problem

Misophonia affects an estimated 5% to 20% of the population, yet audiology lacks standardized clinical pathways or objective behavioral metrics to measure its functional impact on speech understanding. Standard diagnostic tools require specialized laboratory equipment and professional supervision, making them unscalable for population-level screening and early intervention.

## Method

SRM is structured as a six-minute space mission accessible via any modern web browser using only headphones. The pipeline includes an onboarding phase with a guided audio check, an adaptive 1-up/1-down staircase procedure using VCV syllable tokens to estimate the Speech Reception Threshold (SRT) in amplitude-modulated noise, and a personalized masker selection stage where users rate everyday annoyance triggers. Finally, participants undergo a blocked speech identification task across three masker conditions: a self-selected annoying sound (Premium Fuel), an envelope-matched synthetic noise (Synthetic Fuel), and standard amplitude-modulated noise (Standard Fuel).

## Results

In a pilot study of 14 participants, the tool achieved a 100% completion rate with a mean session time of 6.5 minutes. Adaptive thresholds converged reliably with a mean SRT of -9.4 dB SNR. Among participants with full comparison data, speech intelligibility showed a clear performance gradient, dropping to 59.2% under the personalized Premium Fuel compared to 71.7% for Synthetic Fuel and 86.3% for Standard Fuel.

## Applications

Audiologists, hearing healthcare providers, and researchers can use this web-based tool for scalable misophonia screening, remote hearing care engagement, and evaluating how emotional sound sensitivities impact speech perception.

## Limitations

Some participants exhibited very low SRT thresholds between -34 and -38 dB SNR, pointing to potential ceiling effects that necessitate more challenging maskers for high-performing listeners in future versions.
