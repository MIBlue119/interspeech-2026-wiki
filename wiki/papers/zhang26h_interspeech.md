# Gender differences in the phonetic realization of the checked tone in Kaihui Xiang

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26h_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26h_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-691)

**TL;DR** — This study investigates gender-based acoustic differences in the checked tone (T6) of Kaihui Xiang to examine ongoing de-checking, demonstrating complete coda loss alongside stable F0 contours and reorganized voice quality cues.

## Problem

Checked tones in Chinese languages often undergo smoothing or de-checking, yet the phonetic mechanisms and sociophonetic conditioning driving this attrition remain understudied. Specifically, the role of gender in shaping the de-checking trajectory is largely unknown, as women frequently lead sound changes in progress. To address this, the authors examine Kaihui Xiang, an understudied suburban subvariety of Changsha Xiang hypothesized to be undergoing an intermediate stage of this process.

## Method

The study conducted a controlled production experiment with 16 native speakers of Kaihui Xiang (9 female, 7 male; aged 42–80) reading 104 monosyllabic target words embedded in carrier sentences, yielding 2,792 analyzed tokens after screening. Four acoustic dimensions were extracted using VoiceSauce: coda presence, F0, vowel duration, and phonation measures ($H_1^* - H_2^*$ and $\text{HNR}_{05}$). Statistical analysis utilized Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LMMs) for vowel duration and Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMMs) with dummy-variable decomposition to capture non-linear temporal trajectories across time, gender, and tone type.

## Results

The dataset comprised 2,792 tokens from 16 speakers across six tonal categories. Results show oral stop codas are absent or inconsistently realized, yet the checked tone (T6) remains distinct via a stable falling F0 contour invariant across genders ($p = 0.972$). Duration is significantly shorter for the checked tone ($\beta = -0.45, p < 0.001$), with male speakers exhibiting stronger durational reduction while female speakers show weaker temporal cues ($\beta = 0.06, p = 0.025$). Voice quality measures reveal a leftward shift of laryngealization rather than final-phase creakiness, with female speakers showing significantly lower $H_1^* - H_2^*$ values at syllable onset indicating more compressed phonation.

## Applications

## Limitations

The study only examines a single prosodic configuration, precluding evaluation of cue stability across different structural environments, and lacks younger speakers to enable a direct apparent-time comparison for confirming ongoing change.
