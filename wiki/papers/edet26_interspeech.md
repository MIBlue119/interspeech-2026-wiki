# Towards Digital Preservation of Efik: TTS for a Low-Resource African Language

**TL;DR** — The first documented TTS system for Efik, a tonal Nigerian language with millions of speakers but no prior speech-synthesis research, built and evaluated with a new single-speaker corpus.

## Problem

Efik, a tonal language spoken by roughly 3 million second-language and 1.5 million native speakers in Southeastern Nigeria, remains underrepresented in speech synthesis research, leaving it at risk in digital-preservation terms.

## Method

The authors build a curated single-speaker Efik corpus (2,632 utterances, about three hours) and comparatively evaluate four neural TTS models — VITS, MMS-TTS, SpeechT5, and Orpheus-TTS — under low-resource conditions, with native speakers rating outputs via MOS, Nat-MOS, and A-MOS.

## Results

MMS-TTS achieves the highest MOS (3.80 ± 0.63) and produces more stable long-form speech, though tonal errors persist; other models show greater tonal and prosodic inconsistency.

## Applications

Language documentation and preservation efforts, plus accessibility tools (e.g. screen readers) for Efik and similarly under-resourced tonal African languages.
