# Beyond Pitch: Multidimensional Cue Reweighting of Two High-Falling Tones in Pingdingshan Mandarin

**TL;DR** — A phonetic study showing that as a Mandarin dialect's two high-falling tones lose their pitch distinction across generations, younger speakers compensate by leaning more on phonation and duration cues.

## Problem

Little is documented about "reverse tonogenesis," where a degraded pitch contrast causes other acoustic cues to be reweighted, as opposed to the classic path from consonant voicing to pitch distinctions.

## Method

Analyzes speech and EGG recordings from 32 speakers of different ages producing two high-falling tones in Pingdingshan Mandarin, applying mixed-effects models and random forest analyses over pitch, phonation, duration, and energy cues.

## Results

Older speakers distinguish the tones mainly through pitch; younger speakers, whose pitch contrast is narrowing, increasingly rely on creaky phonation and duration instead.
