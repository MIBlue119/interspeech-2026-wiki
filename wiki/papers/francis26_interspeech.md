# No-Shot Text-to-Speech: Limitations of Zero-Shot TTS and its Evaluation Methods in Representing Queer and Transgender Voices

**TL;DR** — Testing six zero-shot TTS systems on gender-expansive versus non-gender-expansive speakers finds inconsistent quality gaps, and shows automated evaluation metrics disagree with human judgments and each other.

## Problem

Gender-expansive (queer and transgender) voices are underrepresented in speech datasets, raising concerns about how well zero-shot TTS systems and their evaluation methods generalize to and fairly represent these speakers.

## Method

The authors evaluate six zero-shot TTS models on two datasets — one of non-gender-expansive (N-GE) speakers and one of self-identified gender-expansive (GE) speakers — comparing human similarity ratings against automated speaker-similarity and MOS-prediction metrics.

## Results

Two of six models synthesize less similar voices for GE speakers while three perform better on GE voices; automated evaluations conflict with each other and with human ratings inconsistently across models, exposing a blind spot in current TTS evaluation pipelines.
