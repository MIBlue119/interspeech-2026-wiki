# Predicting Cognitive Load from Speech and Interaction Dynamics in Dyadic Conversations

**TL;DR** — Turn-taking and participation-balance patterns in natural two-person conversation predict perceived cognitive load, extending lab-based cognitive-load estimation to real collaborative settings.

## Problem

Estimating cognitive load from speech has mostly been studied in controlled laboratory settings, leaving its reliability in natural collaborative conversation poorly understood.

## Method

The authors analyze audio from 53 dyads performing nine collaborative tasks, extract static acoustic, dynamic, and interaction (turn-taking) features, and train a two-head Gated Recurrent Unit encoder to predict perceived cognitive load scores.

## Results

Conversational interaction features usefully predict cognitive load facets such as time pressure, mental effort, and task performance; temporal demand ties to turn-taking dynamics like overlap and speaker switch, while mental demand ties to imbalanced participation between speakers.
