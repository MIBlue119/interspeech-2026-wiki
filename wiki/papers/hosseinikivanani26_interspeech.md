# Speaker or Language? Explaining Variance in Charismatic Prosody Across Luxembourgish and French

**TL;DR** — Analyzing bilingual politicians' speeches shows that speaker identity explains most of the variance in charismatic prosody, but language still leaves a systematic fingerprint — French favors a polite institutional tone, Luxembourgish a vocally present, informal one.

## Problem

Charismatic speech is shaped by both language and speaking style, but their relative contributions in bilingual public speaking are unclear.

## Method

The authors analyze spontaneous speeches from 10 politicians addressing audiences in both Luxembourgish and French in comparable contexts, extracting 41 acoustic-prosodic charisma features from 400 utterances and fitting mixed-effects models to separate speaker- from language-related variance.

## Results

Speaker identity accounts for most variance, but language still shows systematic differences: French productions have higher shimmer and phrase-final F0 (a polite, respectful voice), while Luxembourgish productions show stronger mid-frequency spectral energy (a more vocally present profile), aligning with each language's sociolinguistic role.
