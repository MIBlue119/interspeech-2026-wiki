# Indigenising Speech Technology: Building a TTS Model for te Reo Māori

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/leoni26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/leoni26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1443)

**TL;DR** — This paper presents an Indigenous-led data curation and collection methodology for building a high-quality, bilingual text-to-speech (TTS) model for te reo Māori and New Zealand English using roughly 32 hours of meticulously curated speech data.

## Problem

Mainstream speech technology frequently fails to produce high-quality TTS outputs for Indigenous and low-resource languages because available open-source datasets are poorly curated, ignore unique phonetic and orthographic characteristics, and lack accent diversity. This absence of representative synthetic voices excludes native speakers from accessing everyday technologies and limits tools available for language revitalization. Addressing this requires an Indigenous-led framework that prioritizes data sovereignty, community trust, and rigorous quality assurance over sheer dataset volume.

## Method

The development pipeline centers on strict data sovereignty, explicit consent, and deep community involvement by partnering with native-speaking language specialists from local tribes. Curated text sources encompass both fiction and non-fiction authored by Māori for Māori, which are manually reviewed, edited, segmented, and updated for correct orthography and macrons. Voice talent comprises vetted native-speaking broadcasters with strong genealogical ties to the community, recording in professional studio environments alongside experienced producers. The final dataset totals approximately 24 hours for the male voice (nearly 11 hours Māori, over 13 hours English) and over 8 hours for the female voice (over 3 hours Māori, nearly 5 hours English). Audio files undergo iterative specialist review for cadence, pronunciation, and iterative gap-filling (such as acronyms and numbers).

## Results

The resulting datasets comprise ~24 hours of male voice data and >8 hours of female voice data, spanning both te reo Māori and New Zealand English. The study demonstrates that meticulous manual curation, orthographic correction, and expert human quality assurance yield high-fidelity, natural-sounding synthesis without requiring massive web-scraped corpora. Qualitative analyses confirm successful capture of language-specific phonetic structures like unique consonant combinations and vowel sequences.

## Limitations

The work currently focuses on two specific speaker voices and requires intensive manual oversight from expert language specialists.
