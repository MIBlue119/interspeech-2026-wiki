# Closing the Speech-Text Gap with Limited Audio for Effective Domain Adaptation in LLM-Based ASR

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/banerasroux26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/banerasroux26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3383)

**TL;DR** — A mixed-batch domain adaptation strategy combining abundant text data with as little as 10% target-domain speech (<4 hours) matches or exceeds the performance of full-data ASR fine-tuning in LLM-based speech recognition.

## Problem

End-to-end ASR systems adapted via text-only fine-tuning suffer from a modality gap because the language model loses exposure to the noisy, continuous representations generated by upstream speech projectors. While target-domain text is typically abundant in specialized domains like finance or agriculture, paired speech data is scarce and expensive to gather. Standard text adaptation alone leads to cross-modal misalignment and catastrophic forgetting of the acoustic-text mapping.

## Method

The architecture follows the SLAM-ASR framework, connecting a frozen WavLM-Large speech encoder through a trainable linear speech projector to a Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct LLM, using LoRA adapters for parameter-efficient adaptation. The mixed batching (MB) training strategy combines source-domain data (paired audio-text, projector-induced noisy tokens, and synthetically corrupted transcripts) with target-domain data consisting of paired target speech-text and corrupted target transcripts. This setup keeps adaptation grounded in real acoustics while leveraging plentiful text supervision. Models are trained for 5 epochs with a batch size of 10, setting the total target batch proportion to 50%.

## Results

Evaluated on DefinedAI Banking (in-domain) and SlideSpeech Agriculture and Musical Instruments (out-of-domain) datasets, comparing text-only adaptation, standard speech-text fine-tuning, and mixed batching. On the Banking domain, standard paired speech-text adaptation yields a WER of 4.55% (compared to an 8.02% baseline), whereas text-only adaptation yields 6.38%. Using the proposed mixed batching strategy with only 10% of target audio (corresponding to 3h37 for Banking, 2h55 for Agriculture, and 50m for Musical Instruments) achieves word error rates comparable to or better than full-data fine-tuning.

## Applications

Speech and ML engineers building domain-adapted automatic speech recognition systems for low-resource or specialized vertical sectors (such as finance, agriculture, or healthcare) where text documents are plentiful but audio recordings are scarce.
