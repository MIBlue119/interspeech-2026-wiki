# Decolonizing Linguistic Policies in Automatic Speech Recognition: A Framework for Cross-Culturally Competent Speech AI

**TL;DR** — A framework paper arguing that ASR failures on low-resource and Indigenous languages are implicit linguistic policies, not just technical bugs, and proposing a participatory audit protocol to fix them.

## Problem

ASR systems and voice interfaces persistently fail for low-resource, Indigenous, and non-standard language varieties, shaping unequal access to public services, healthcare, and education; the authors argue these failures function as implicit linguistic policies that reproduce colonial language hierarchies.

## Method

Drawing on linguistic capital, raciolinguistic ideology, language policy research, and decolonial computing, the paper analyzes how data, metrics, and model priors determine whose voice becomes machine-legible, introducing a "Three Harms" (Misrecognition, Misalignment, Mistrust) taxonomy and a seven-layer situatedness model for linguistic diversity in ASR.

## Results

This is a conceptual/framework contribution rather than an empirical study: it proposes a participatory framework and a minimum audit protocol for culturally competent ASR that positions affected communities as co-designers, evaluators, and governance partners.
