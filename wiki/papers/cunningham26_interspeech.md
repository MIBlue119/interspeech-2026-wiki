# Decolonizing Linguistic Policies in Automatic Speech Recognition: A Framework for Cross-Culturally Competent Speech AI

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/cunningham26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/cunningham26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-3351)

**TL;DR** — This paper establishes a conceptual and evaluative framework for culturally competent automated speech recognition by diagnosing how technical design choices reproduce colonial linguistic hierarchies.

## Problem

Speech AI systems routinely fail speakers of low-resource, Indigenous, and non-standard language varieties, blocking access to vital public services, healthcare, and education. The authors argue that these persistent failures are not mere technical bugs, but implicit linguistic policies embedded in data curation, evaluation metrics, and model priors that reproduce colonial language hierarchies. Consequently, marginalized speakers face systemic exclusion and the indignity of being rendered machine-illegible by narrow institutional standards.

## Method

The authors synthesize linguistic capital theory, raciolinguistic ideology, language policy research, and decolonial computing to conceptualize ASR design as linguistic policy. They introduce the Three Harms (3M) taxonomy (Misrecognition, Misalignment, and Mistrust) and a seven-layer situatedness model for linguistic diversity spanning from base language labels to socio-technical implications. Building on these concepts, they propose a participatory governance framework and a minimum audit protocol that positions affected language communities as co-designers, evaluators, and partners.

## Results

As a theoretical and human-centered evaluation paper, no new ASR neural architecture or empirical benchmark dataset is introduced. Instead, the authors provide a diagnostic methodology and audit protocol to uncover harms that standard Word Error Rate (WER) evaluations under-specify. The framework is demonstrated through connections to existing multilingual and regional speech corpora and literature across African, American, and global linguistic communities.

## Limitations

The work provides a conceptual evaluation and governance framework rather than an automated computational drop-in module or algorithmic fix.
