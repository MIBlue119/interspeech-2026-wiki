# Intonation Perception in Real and Synthetic Speech across Varying Familiarity Levels: A Pilot Study of Equivalence Assessment

**TL;DR** — A pilot study comparing listener perception of natural versus singing-voice-conversion-synthesized speech finds that intonation-based cues for speaker identification and question recognition are influenced by both synthetic artifacts and listener familiarity.

## Problem

Voice-cloning-based synthetic speech offers a low-cost way to build language-training corpora, but it isn't well established how equivalent listener perception of synthetic speech is to natural speech, particularly for intonation-based cues.

## Method

Using a singing voice conversion model to generate synthetic voices, the authors compare participant performance on natural versus synthetic speech across two experiments: a similarity perception task and an intonation recognition task, examining interactions with speech type, intonation, and listener familiarity.

## Results

In the similarity perception task, a significant interaction between speech type and intonation suggests question intonation can cue speaker identification but is affected by synthetic features; in the intonation recognition task, a significant interaction between speech type and familiarity shows speech type affects how much listener familiarity contributes to voice processing.
