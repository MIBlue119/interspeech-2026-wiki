# Intonation Perception in Real and Synthetic Speech across Varying Familiarity Levels: A Pilot Study of Equivalence Assessment

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhou26b_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhou26b_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-996)

**TL;DR** — This study evaluates human perception of natural versus AI-generated synthetic speech across varying speaker familiarity levels and intonation patterns, finding significant interactions between speech type, intonation, and familiarity.

## Problem

Constructing high-quality speech corpora for language training is resource-intensive, prompting interest in AI-powered voice clones, but current evaluations focus mainly on declarative sentences while overlooking complex prosodic features like intonation. It remains unclear how synthetic prosody and speaker familiarity jointly modulate human speech perception, voice identification, and linguistic processing.

## Method

The pilot study uses a 2x2x2 within-subject design examining speech type (natural vs. synthetic generated via an F0-conditioned singing voice conversion model), speaker familiarity (familiar acquaintances vs. strange speakers), and intonation (statement vs. question). Stimuli consist of 10 Mandarin neutral sentences recorded by 4 speakers in a soundproof booth and evaluated by 17 adult participants across two tasks (speaker identity similarity perception and intonation recognition). Generalized linear mixed-effects models, CLMMs, and Firth's bias-reduced logistic regressions analyze response accuracy, reaction times, and 7-point Likert similarity scores.

## Results

In the similarity perception task, accuracy was significantly higher for pairs of natural speeches (Mean = 0.917) compared to mixed natural-synthetic pairs (Mean = 0.851, p < 0.001), with a significant interaction showing question intonation enhanced natural speech identification accuracy (Mean = 0.946 vs 0.875, p = 0.046). Familiar speaker voices yielded significantly faster reaction times (Mean = 0.748s vs 0.848s, p = 0.010) and lower similarity ratings (Mean = 3.560 vs 3.930, p = 0.018) than strange voices. In the intonation recognition task, a ceiling effect was observed (accuracies > 95%), while reaction times showed questions took significantly longer than statements (Mean = 0.270s vs 0.249s, p = 0.050).

## Applications

Speech and language researchers, educators, and developers building high-quality corpora for language training and intervention programs using AI voice cloning.

## Limitations

The study is a small pilot involving only 17 participants and a limited set of Mandarin sentences, resulting in ceiling effects for the intonation recognition task.
