# Neural Oscillatory Mechanisms of Speaker Normalization Under Cognitive Load: Evidence from Cantonese Tone Perception

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26ba_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/zhang26ba_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1940)

**TL;DR** — This paper investigates the neural mechanisms of speaker normalization under cognitive load using EEG, revealing that behavioral tone perception constancy is maintained via compensatory parieto-occipital alpha synchronization and prefrontal delta desynchronization.

## Problem

A persistent empirical paradox in speech perception exists regarding speaker normalization: while behavioral studies show that extrinsic normalization resists cognitive depletion from concurrent tasks, cognitive theories suggest normalization is an active, resource-demanding control process. This paper investigates how the brain's neural oscillation networks dynamically reconfigure to maintain speaker adaptation when cognitive resources are scarce.

## Method

The study employed a dual-task paradigm combining an auditory Cantonese tone word identification task (using context-target pairs with F0-shifted speaker cues) with a secondary visual search task of varying complexity. Thirty-two native Cantonese speakers (30 analyzed after EEG artifact rejection) participated in three conditions: no load (NL), low-load visual grid (LL, 4x4), and high-load visual grid (HL, 8x8). EEG data were recorded across 64 channels, and Event-Related Spectral Perturbations (ERSP) were computed via Fast Fourier Transform to analyze time-frequency responses in alpha (8-13 Hz) and delta (1-3 Hz) bands.

## Results

Behavioral expected response accuracy showed a significant main effect of Context F0 (high-F0 accuracy significantly higher than low- and mid-F0, p < 0.001), but no significant main effect of Cognitive Load (p = 0.85) or interaction, demonstrating that tone normalization remained robust despite visual distractions. Time-frequency analysis revealed that while the no-load condition exhibited early prefrontal and parieto-occipital alpha-band desynchronization (0-415 ms), visual load conditions triggered sustained parieto-occipital alpha synchronization (LL: 246-800 ms, HL: 159-800 ms). Furthermore, high load uniquely induced prefrontal delta-band desynchronization (303-565 ms). A significant negative correlation (r = -0.38, p = 0.036) between secondary task accuracy and alpha power in the LL condition supported the inhibitory gating hypothesis.

## Applications

## Limitations

The study tested only Cantonese speakers with visual distractors, and no significant beta-band activity was observed to support context maintenance via working memory.
