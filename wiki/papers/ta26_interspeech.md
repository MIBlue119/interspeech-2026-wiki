# Age-related Differences in the Perception of Vowel Length Contrast in Northern Vietnamese: The Case of Hoang Van (Bac Ninh) Variety

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/ta26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/ta26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1167)

**TL;DR** — A perceptual study on Northern Vietnamese vowel length contrasts reveals that while both duration and spectral cues guide listeners, younger speakers rely more heavily on duration, pointing to an ongoing sound change.

## Problem

Phonological vowel length contrasts typically rely on multiple acoustic cues, but the relative perceptual weighting of duration versus spectral cues (F1, F2) remains under-studied within single speech communities. Furthermore, comparing listener generations using apparent-time approaches is vital for uncovering the perceptual origins of sound change, yet prior work on Northern Vietnamese lacked controlled community-wide data. This gap obscures how cues are integrated and whether cue hierarchies shift across generations.

## Method

The authors conducted a two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) identification experiment with 41 native speakers of the Hoang Van variety (Bac Ninh, Vietnam), divided into younger (mean age 24) and older (mean age 52.5) cohorts. Base tokens of /ta:t 45/ and /tat 45/ were manipulated via Praat to build a 5-step duration continuum, 3-step F1 continuum, and 3-step F2 continuum (90 target stimuli plus 30 fillers). Mixed-effects logistic regression models and linear mixed-effects models were fitted to analyze identification responses and log-transformed response times, examining the main effects and interactions of vowel duration, F1, F2, and listener generation.

## Results

Across all participants, duration emerged as the strongest perceptual cue (log-odds = 1.22, p < 0.001), followed by F1 (0.97, p < 0.001) and F2 (0.15, p = 0.004). Younger listeners exhibited significantly greater sensitivity to duration cues than older listeners (duration × generation interaction: β = 0.25, p < 0.001). At the individual level, all younger listeners used duration primarily, whereas 6 out of 21 older listeners relied predominantly on F1 instead of duration. Response time analyses showed that acoustically incongruent stimuli (e.g., long duration paired with short vowel quality) elicited significantly slower responses overall (β = 0.15, p = 0.001), with younger groups responding faster overall than older groups (β = 0.51, p < 0.001).

## Applications

Phoneticians, sociolinguists, and speech researchers studying sound change mechanisms, cue weighting, and perceptual processing models in tonal or vowel-length languages.

## Limitations

The perception experiment utilized a single male speaker's voice, and production data from the same community were not simultaneously analyzed.
