# Reconciling Dynamic Data Analysis with Linguistic Reality: Comparing Legendre Polynomial Modelling and GAMM Applied to Prosodic Contact

**TL;DR** — Compares two statistical methods for modeling intonation contours on Cypriot vs. Athenian Greek continuation rises, finding they agree on the underlying contact-induced patterns but reveal different aspects of them.

## Problem

Researchers lack clarity on how different dynamic-curve modeling methods for intonation compare when applied to real contact-induced dialectal variation, here continuation rises in Cypriot Greek shaped by contact with Athenian Greek.

## Method

The authors fit both Legendre Polynomial modelling and Generalized Additive Mixed Modelling (GAMM) to pitch-contour data from young speakers of Cypriot and Athenian Greek and compare what each method reveals about the contours.

## Results

Both methods identify the same two Cypriot Greek contour patterns (an Athenian-like low-nuclear contour and a high-nuclear variant with different slope/curvature), but polynomial modelling better captures global contour shape while GAMM better resolves timing differences near the nuclear pitch target.
