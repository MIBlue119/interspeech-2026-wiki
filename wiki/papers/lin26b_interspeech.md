# The Role of Context and Prosody in the Understanding of English Irony by Chinese L2 Learners

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/lin26b_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/lin26b_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-739)

**TL;DR** — This study investigates how context and prosody jointly influence L2 irony comprehension among Chinese English learners, revealing that contextual cues play a dominant role while prosodic features act as an independent backup when context is ambiguous.

## Problem

Irony inherently involves a mismatch between literal expression and intended meaning, making it difficult for second language (L2) learners to interpret. While native speakers' use of context and prosody is well-studied, their relative contributions and interaction during L2 pragmatic processing remain unclear, alongside debate over how much language proficiency modulates this ability.

## Method

A perception experiment was conducted with 48 Chinese undergraduate learners of English divided into high and low-proficiency groups via a C-test. Participants listened to 486 English stimuli generated by combining 61 literal praising target sentences (“X+be+really+adj.”) with three context types (strong incongruent, ambiguous, non-incongruent) and three prosodic styles (ironic, neutral, sincere) created using a combination of AI text-to-speech contexts and human recordings. Responses and reaction times were collected using a two-alternative forced-choice task in PsychoPy, and data were analyzed via Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMM) in R.

## Results

Strong incongruent contexts elicited the highest overall rate of ironic responses (83.80%), followed by ambiguous (38.33%) and non-incongruent contexts (22.80%). Ironic prosody produced the highest irony recognition rate (66.32%), particularly dominating in ambiguous contexts (75.47% ironic responses versus 29.46% for neutral and 10.97% for sincere). Language proficiency showed no overall main effect on response rates, though high-level learners exhibited slightly higher sensitivity in strong incongruent contexts (93.9% vs 83.9%) and under ironic prosody (74.8% vs 64.2%).

## Applications

Applied linguists, speech therapists, and English language educators designing curricula for second-language pragmatic competence and nuanced listening comprehension.

## Limitations

The study relied exclusively on behavioral perception data (response proportions and reaction times) without neural or eye-tracking measures, and used literal praising target sentences exclusively.
