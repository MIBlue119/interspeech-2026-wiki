# The Role of Context and Prosody in the Understanding of English Irony by Chinese L2 Learners

**TL;DR** — A perception experiment finds contextual information dominates over prosody in helping Chinese L2 English learners detect irony, with prosody mattering mainly when context is limited, and proficiency having only a minor effect.

## Problem

Ironic interpretation relies heavily on contextual and prosodic cues, but how these two factors interact for second-language (L2) listeners, and whether proficiency modulates this, remains unclear.

## Method

The authors run a perception experiment using English materials that cross three contextual conditions with three prosodic conditions, testing Chinese English learners' irony comprehension.

## Results

Context plays a dominant role in irony comprehension, while prosody contributes independently mainly when contextual information is limited; language proficiency has only a minor effect, supporting the Parallel Constraint Satisfaction Model.
