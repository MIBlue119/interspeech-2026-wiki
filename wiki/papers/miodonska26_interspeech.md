# Age-dependent acoustic changes of the frication noise in dental sibilants produced by typically developing Polish children between 5 and 8 years of age

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/miodonska26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/miodonska26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-799)

**TL;DR** — This study analyzes how acoustic properties of Polish dental sibilants /s, z/ change with age in typically developing children, revealing age-dependent shifts in fricative formants and high-frequency noise bands indicative of maturing articulatory precision.

## Problem

While sibilant acquisition is critical in early childhood and speech therapy, existing knowledge regarding subtle, continuous acoustic developmental changes in typically developing children remains sparse. Most acoustic research on sibilants targets adults or pathological deviations rather than establishing precise normative baselines for preschool and early school-aged children. Understanding these age-related patterns is essential for accurate clinical diagnosis and effective speech therapy interventions.

## Method

The authors analyzed speech samples from 88 typically developing Polish children aged 61 to 96 months who demonstrated target dental sibilant articulation. The dataset comprises 14 words containing dental fricatives /s, z/ recorded at 44.1 kHz. Signals were segmented using STFT with 20 ms frames and 10 ms overlap, extracting 12 whole-spectrum features and 29 frication noise features (including fricative formants FF1-FF4, formant distances, peak frequencies, and subband energies NE0-NE9). Linear mixed-effect models were constructed in MATLAB to evaluate age and sex as fixed effects while accounting for speaker and word-level random intercepts and slopes.

## Results

Across 12 general spectral features and speaker sex, no statistically significant effects were observed for either /s/ or /z/. However, linear mixed-effect models revealed significant age-related changes: for unvoiced /s/, the second-to-first formant distance (FFD12) and high-frequency energy bands NE8 and NE9 (6–7 kHz) significantly increased with age. For voiced /z/, the first fricative formant (FF1) and low-frequency energy band NE1 (2.0–2.5 kHz) significantly decreased with age. These acoustic shifts point toward a widening spectral peak structure and a relative reduction of low-frequency noise components as children grow older.

## Applications

Speech-language pathologists and researchers in developmental phonetics can use these normative acoustic baselines to better diagnose articulation disorders and assess speech therapy progress in children.

## Limitations

The study did not fully account for potential confounding variations between spontaneously elicited words and repeated words, and the participant pool was limited to monolingual Polish speakers.
