# Age-dependent acoustic changes of the frication noise in dental sibilants produced by typically developing Polish children between 5 and 8 years of age

**TL;DR** — Spectral properties of Polish children's /s/ and /z/ sounds shift systematically with age, suggesting a measurable acoustic signature of maturing articulatory precision between ages 5 and 8.

## Problem

It isn't well characterized which acoustic properties of children's dental fricative production relate to age and sex during typical development, information needed as a baseline for detecting disordered sibilant production.

## Method

The authors analyze speech from 88 Polish children aged 61-96 months producing target dental /s, z/, extracting spectral features of the frication noise and fitting linear mixed-effect models against age and sex.

## Results

Fricative formant distance and spectral energy above 6kHz significantly increase with age for /s/, while spectral energy below 2.5kHz significantly decreases with age for /z/, indicating an age-dependent spectral shift consistent with improving articulatory precision.
