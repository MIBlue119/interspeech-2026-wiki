# VoxENES 2026: Benchmarking Generalization of Speech Spoofing Detectors Against LLM-Era TTS and Voice Conversion

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/sharma26b_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/sharma26b_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2712)

**TL;DR** — VoxENES 2026 is a bilingual benchmark dataset of 53,628 audio samples covering modern LLM-driven TTS, voice conversion, and postprocessing, revealing that eight pretrained spoofing detectors suffer severe performance degradation with the best achieving only 28.98% EER.

## Problem

Legacy speech spoofing datasets like ASVspoof rely on synthesis methods predating 2024 and fail to capture acoustic patterns generated by recent large language model (LLM)-driven TTS and voice conversion systems. This temporal mismatch causes existing detectors to learn brittle artifacts tied to older pipelines, leading to an overestimation of detector robustness under real-world deployment conditions and postprocessing manipulations.

## Method

The authors introduce VoxENES 2026, comprising 3,028 real speech samples from LibriSpeech and VoxPopuli (English and Spanish) combined with 50,600 synthetic samples generated across seven modern TTS engines (e.g., VoxCPM 1.5, Qwen3-TTS, GLM-TTS, FlashLabs Chroma, VibeVoice, CosyVoice 3, Chatterbox ML) and three voice conversion systems (Seed-VC, OpenVoice v2, RVC v2). All audio is standardized to 16 kHz mono WAV with 4-second fixed-length truncation/zero-padding. Furthermore, the synthetic set undergoes ten standardized postprocessing augmentations (MP3/AAC compression, white/babble noise, resampling, speed perturbations, and volume normalization). Eight diverse pretrained deepfake detectors—spanning graph neural networks, raw-waveform CNNs, SSL classifiers, spectrogram transformers, and speaker embedding anomaly detectors—are evaluated zero-shot without fine-tuning.

## Results

Evaluated on VoxENES 2026, the best-performing detector (AST-ASVspoof5) achieves an overall EER of 28.98%, while five of the eight evaluated detectors perform at or below random chance (EER >= 47%), with AASIST2 reaching 57.86% due to inverted prediction behavior caused by domain mismatch. Seed-VC is identified as the most challenging synthesis method, yielding EERs above 41% across all detectors due to its diffusion-based architecture preserving naturalistic features. Postprocessing effects are non-uniform: white noise reduces EER for AST-ASVspoof5 from 26.7% to 17.4% by attenuating generator artifacts and introducing alternative cues, whereas MP3 compression degrades its EER to 48.4%.

## Applications

Speech/ML engineers and security practitioners can use this benchmark to evaluate, stress-test, and develop robust audio deepfake detection countermeasures against modern LLM-driven synthesis and realistic transmission distortions.

## Limitations

The evaluation is restricted to zero-shot inference without fine-tuning on the benchmark, and the study leaves detailed analysis of zero-padding artifacts for future work.
