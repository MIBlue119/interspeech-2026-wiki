# From Words to Sentences: Contextual Predictability Overrides Phonetic Ambiguity in Lexical Competition

**TL;DR** — The graded effect of phonetic ambiguity on word recognition found in isolated words disappears once a predictive sentence context is present, suggesting top-down expectation can override bottom-up acoustic detail.

## Problem

Prior work shows that sub-phonemic acoustic variation modulates lexical competition in a graded way for isolated words, but whether this effect persists when sentence context is available was unknown.

## Method

The authors run two cross-modal priming experiments with acoustically manipulated English minimal pairs — one with isolated words, one with the same targets embedded in high- or low-predictability sentences — and compare how phonetic ambiguity and context jointly affect lexical competition.

## Results

Isolated-word recognition replicates the graded phonetic-ambiguity effect, but in sentences, competition is better resolved by predictability while no longer being modulated by phonetic ambiguity, consistent with predictive-coding accounts where top-down expectation reduces reliance on the bottom-up signal.
