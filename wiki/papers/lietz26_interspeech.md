# Making Room for Speech Diversity: A 50 Year Retrospective of Speech Science and Technology through a Neurodivergent Lens

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/lietz26_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/lietz26_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-2962)

**TL;DR** — This paper conducts a 50-year scoping review of neurodivergent speech literature across Interspeech and ICASSP, revealing prevailing medicalized framings, minimal stakeholder inclusion, and ableist language practices.

## Problem

Speech technologies routinely underperform for diverse speech populations because they cater primarily to typical patterns. Examining how speech research frames diversity is critical, yet historical discourses often pathologize neurodivergence rather than addressing structural accessibility barriers. This scoping review evaluates how neurodiversity has been approached over the past five decades to guide more inclusive future practices.

## Method

The authors perform a scoping review and content/discourse analysis of 117 papers published between 1976 and 2025 across Interspeech, ICASSP, ICSLP, and EuroSpeech. Papers were retrieved using targeted keyword stems and filtered to focus on developmental neurodivergent conditions affecting speech, such as stuttering, autism spectrum disorder (ASD), bipolar disorder, Down syndrome, and ADHD. The analysis examines research motivations, data practices, evaluation frameworks, stakeholder involvement, and linguistic framing through the lens of disability models (medical vs. social).

## Results

Out of an initial corpus of 1170 papers, 117 met all inclusion criteria, spanning 75 Interspeech, 33 ICASSP, 5 ICSLP, and 4 EuroSpeech publications. Stuttering (52 papers) and autism (50 papers) represent the vast majority of studies, with research interest surging post-2010 to reach nearly 0.8% of all Interspeech publications in recent years. Stuttering papers grew rapidly from 14% of the corpus in 2016-2020 to nearly 62% between 2021 and 2025, largely driven by tasks like detection and automatic recognition. Conversely, ADHD (1 paper) and Down syndrome (3 papers) remain severely underrepresented.

## Applications

Speech and machine learning researchers, dataset creators, and conference organizers use these insights to design anti-ableist, participatory speech technologies in collaboration with disability communities.

## Limitations

The review focuses specifically on developmental neurodivergent conditions and restricts its primary corpus to Interspeech and ICASSP venues.
