# Modulation of Phonetic Realizations in Cantonese Dialogue with Human and AI Interlocutors

[PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chen26m_interspeech.pdf) · [ISCA page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/chen26m_interspeech.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2026-1194)

**TL;DR** — This study investigates fine-grained phonetic modulation in Cantonese dialogue when speakers interact with human versus AI interlocutors under neutral and negative emotional stances, revealing shorter target word durations and tone-specific F0 adjustments in AI interactions.

## Problem

Prior work on phonetic accommodation in human-AI interaction has focused primarily on intonation languages and positively valenced or prosocial systems, leaving a gap in understanding how users adapt their speech in tone languages when facing synthetic or negatively expressive ('antagonistic') AI interlocutors. Because task types and linguistic structures vary, it remains unclear whether interacting with synthetic agents triggers global adaptations or more localized, fine-grained phonetic modifications in a tonal system like Cantonese.

## Method

Seventeen native Cantonese speakers completed 20 scripted interactive dialogues (alternating turns) in a Wizard-of-Oz setup, comparing a human model talker via video call with a customized text-to-speech (TTS) AI avatar generated via iPhone Memoji and DurIAN. The customized TTS model was trained on roughly 3,000 sentences from the human talker using duration, pitch, and energy input parameters extracted from human recordings to ensure comparable speech rates and voice qualities. The experiment manipulated Interlocutor Identity (Human vs. AI) and Emotional Stance (Neutral vs. Negative context), measuring utterance-level speech rates, disyllabic target word durations, time-normalized F0 trajectories across all six lexical tones via Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMMs), and F1/F2 corner vowel formants using linear mixed-effects models.

## Results

Analyses revealed a significant main effect of Interlocutor Identity on target word duration, showing shorter target word durations in the AI condition compared to the human condition, while utterance-level speech rates remained unaffected. F0 trajectories demonstrated tone-specific and temporally localized differences: Tone 1 and Tone 4 exhibited significantly raised F0 contours while Tone 5 was lowered when speaking to an AI interlocutor under neutral emotion, and Tone 4 was also raised under negative emotion. Emotional stance showed a marginal effect overall, characterized by a larger vowel space area (VSA) expansion in negative contexts compared to neutral contexts.

## Applications

Speech scientists, conversational AI designers, and human-computer interaction (HCI) engineers can use these findings to understand user accommodation behaviors and build more natural, tone-aware spoken dialogue systems.

## Limitations

The study features a relatively small sample size of 17 participants and uses brief dialogues with rapidly alternating emotional stances, which may have limited the magnitude of observed emotional adaptation effects.
